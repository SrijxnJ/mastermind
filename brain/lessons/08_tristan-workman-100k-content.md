## 2. Frameworks, models, rules of thumb

### Content system (Briar)

**F1. Six pillars of content** [1:04:39–1:06:00] (Briar)
- **The pillars:**
  1. **Strategy:** top/middle/bottom of funnel; are we after virality, views or conversions? Rebalance every week.
  2. **Ideation:** picking topics.
  3. **Scripting & storyboarding:** word-for-word or bullet points.
  4. **Talent management:** called **energy management** when it's your own brand.
  5. **Editing.**
  6. **Growth tactics:** e.g. trial reels. These are named but never explained: "we haven't even got the trial reels".
- **Talent-management options he lists:**
  - a videographer
  - a script or a storyboard
  - mornings
  - a phone call where he "gets it out of them"
  - a teleprompter
  - batching a month at once
  - letting a "yapper" talk for 4–8 min and building the script in the edit [1:27:51]
- Quotes:
  - "there's six pillars to content." [1:04:39]
  - "Energy management is what you call this if it's for yourself." [1:05:28]
- His own-brand note: he cares less about his own content than clients', which is "very freeing". "I have 10 minutes to record. Let me just press play… Sometimes that's my best content." [1:04:23]

**F2. North star + incremental improvement** (Briar, citing Alex Schultz's *Click Here*) [33:40–35:50]
- A great marketer can state the goal in **one sentence**. Different content inputs lead to different north stars, so a clear goal lets you reverse-engineer the inputs.
- Email, ads and landing pages get A/B tested, but content gets treated as a hobby ("day in the life… what I ate this week").
- Quotes:
  - "Give me one sentence." [34:28]
  - Content is "really really dialed marketing but packaged into personality and video." [35:50]

**F3. The four ideation "faucets"** [36:07–39:52] (Briar)
1. **Reddit:** in your niche's subreddit, sort by top of the month, paste a whole thread into ChatGPT and ask what people are talking about. "it's a proven conversation starter that's already happening in your niche." [36:23]
2. **A second TikTok account** that only follows your niche; save videos. **Outlier check:** open the creator's profile and compare the video with their average. 20K views is not an outlier if they average 100K. "make sure it's an outlier for them." [36:55]
3. **Internal data → buzzword:**
   - Take last week's most viral video and summarise its concept as **one specific buzzword**.
   - Make another video on that concept the next week.
   - His example: a Roth IRA / Super Bowl tickets video becomes "creative tax strategies", and the next video is "Rolls-Royce via Section 8 housing". [37:11–38:15]
4. **5 + 5 creators:** 5 competitors in your niche (take their **topics**) and 5 creators outside it (take their **packaging**: formats, camera angles, series). "Pull the topics that your niche competitors are talking about." [38:31]
   - Combining the two is how you "innovate inside of your niche". Others can't copy it because "no one knows where you're taking the combos from." [39:03]
   - His example: Matty G Styles (men's fashion, green screen) as inspiration for business content.

**F4. Untangling data: Hook / Format / Topic / Illustration** [39:52–41:47] (Briar)
- **Split each week's videos into four parts:**
  - **Hook:** the psychological principle ("Did I yell at them? Did I give them a timeline?")
  - **Format:** the visual way it comes across: green screen, podcast, whiteboard, day-in-the-life, top-down.
  - **Topic:** one word or phrase.
  - **Illustration:** how it was communicated: metaphor, story, statistic or quote.
- **Test each part separately the next week.** Keep the parts that still work; drop the ones that don't.
- Quotes:
  - "You improve your content by untangling data." [39:52]
  - "My hook, my format, my topic, and my illustration" [39:52–40:09]
- His claim: the clients who have let him untangle data longest grow fastest. One is "adding 100,000 followers a month". [41:14]

**F5. 1% better per day** [29:33] (Briar)
- Start at 300 views and improve 1% a day through 2026 → "You will be averaging 11,400 views by December 31st, 2026." He says this is "top 0.1% of your niche" and roughly 50K followers.
- *(inferred check: 300 × 1.01³⁶⁵ ≈ 11,330, so the arithmetic holds; the follower and percentile figures are his estimates.)*

**F6. Three blockers + sacred hours** [30:39–32:51] (Briar)
1. **Priority:** believe it's the most important thing after faith and family. He claims he stopped working out for a year to build.
2. **Belief:** it is "as close to a guarantee as any skill there is". [31:47]
3. **Sacred hours:** "Block off an hour time slot um and record content during that time, one time a day." [32:02]
- Ideation is a skill to grow, because you will run out of ideas.
- **Start on TikTok** if you're worried about friends and family on Instagram. Both men say this insecurity cost them "about a year". [32:18]

**F7. Follower quality over virality** [43:57–50:42]
- Briar: "the way you grow and what you grow on is what your audience will always be." [43:57]
  - Stack steady growth for a "kaleidoscope following" instead of one viral spike.
  - "Your life will change with just 10,000 quality followers." [45:01]
- Briar's thought exercise: a 5-minute speech to 130,000 12-year-olds in Michigan Stadium vs a 200 sq ft room with 10 Fortune 500 CEOs. "Not all views or followers are created equally" [47:44]
- Tristan's case: an Ironman series (6 posts in 10 days). One video reached ~1M views. About 1,200 of his ~4,000 followers are "Iron Man people from Sweden" who don't fit his sales content. A podcast with 50 YouTube views brought 4 life-changed DMs. [48:32–50:42]

**F8. Ghost followers and the non-follower view % test** [50:59–54:58] (Briar)
- **How ghost followers form:**
  - Instagram sends a new post to followers first as the test batch.
  - If those followers were won with an off-topic subject, they scroll past.
  - The algorithm reads that as a bad video and stops pushing it to non-followers.
- Quote: "on Instagram, it's a follower first platform" [51:30]
- **His examples:** a Bitcoin creator with 22K followers, 19K of them from his modelling days, who couldn't pass ~1K views. Accounts with 200K followers getting 2–3K views.
- **The test:**
  1. Open your 5–10 most recent videos and look at the non-follower view %.
  2. "If it's above 50%, you're probably fine." [54:11] Elsewhere he says above 60% / 50%.
  3. You then need new followers to outnumber old ones (e.g. 10K mismatched followers means growing to 21K).
  4. Also weigh how fast you're adding followers. If you're gaining 1 a day, consider a new account.
- His own case: 1,500 friends and family followers, adding a couple of hundred a day, so about 3 weeks to outgrow them, and he kept the account.

**F9. What's working (said "December 2025")** [55:14–57:40] (Briar)
- **Clone format** (the same person talking to themselves). "we probably only have a couple weeks left of that before it starts to get overplayed." [55:29]
- **Series** ("day one of doing X"). They give "the most view to follower ratio because people want to come back for more." [1:00:36]
- **Green screen for beginners** instead of the classic mic-in-hand talking head, with "just one image behind you". "green screens are so so good for beginners" [55:45]
- **Substack:** a newsletter that captures emails *and* has discovery built in.
- **Livestreams for educational creators** (his 2026 prediction): e.g. live ideation sessions or a live sales call. He calls it "basically a bakedin webinar" [57:06] and plans two a week.

**F10. Series rules** [58:44–1:01:11] (Briar, with Tristan's marathon example)
- Days can be skipped and needn't be posted in order. The point is that viewers can binge your profile.
- Give value throughout. Don't make it all about you.
- **Blend your expertise into the series angle**, e.g. "day one of using sales call tactics to get business owners to run their first marathon".
- **Raise the stakes** with a forfeit, e.g. a fantasy-league loser swimming in shark water, or "if I don't, I'm running the marathon twice".
- End with "follow along for more".

**F11. Getting over camera nerves** [1:01:42–1:03:20] (Briar)
- *The Inner Game of Tennis*: do the prep, then let your subconscious run ("just have fun").
- "print off a picture of your best friend and put them behind the camera" [1:02:48]. Then say what you'd say to them, and decide later whether to post it.
- Tristan adds that Tim Ferriss wrote *The 4-Hour Workweek* as an email to two friends.

**F12. Month of content in a couple of hours (local-business tier)** [1:25:22–1:28:06] (Briar)
- "we're looking for 14 ideas, 14 formats" [1:25:22–1:25:39]. Run 14 topics for two weeks, then **reformat the same topics** for the next two weeks (green screen → whiteboard → talking head → podcast style).
- His caveat: the feedback loop is worse. "You want feedback loop daily if you can." [1:25:39] He aims for "an 8 out of 10 service" for local businesses.
- His example topic mix for sales:
  - copywriting × sales
  - KPIs
  - managing a team (bottom of funnel)
  - a funny bit ("sell to the Grinch")
  - mental performance
- He uses AI scripting prompts only for this tier ("I hate AI scripting, but… for local businesses, it's probably fine"). [1:27:51]

**F13. AI for ideation, not scripting: "unique, specific, observed"** [1:28:23–1:30:28] (Briar)
- "The way to use AI for content in 2026 has nothing to do with scripting." [1:28:23]
- **His JSON prompt** asks for "the number one most unique, specific, and observed situation that my ideal customer profile has experienced." [1:28:54]
  - **Unique:** not generic.
  - **Specific:** not "watering stuff".
  - **Observed:** they know they lived it.
- His invented gardener example: brown leaves after the first October frost because they forgot to trim.
- **Name the unnamed problem.** He named "follower quality", and big accounts replied "Finally, someone's talking about…" [1:29:57]

**F14. Polarising content: green or red, never grey** [1:30:58–1:33:39] (Briar; the "same enemy" point is Tristan's)
- Take a hard stance on something you actually believe, about 90% of the time. Keep the nuance for private consulting.
- "not drifting into just saying something to piss somebody off that you don't actually believe in" [1:30:58]
- "We don't want to live in the gray." [1:31:48]
- His example take: "building in public is the worst content strategy". People who disagree argue in the comments (which drives reach), and they're also people he doesn't want as clients.
- Tristan: know who you're for and against; a shared enemy is "one of the best copywriting principles". [1:33:39]

**F15. Personal brand = a title + one emotion** [1:34:10–1:36:05] (Briar)
- Tombstone test: what one word or title would people give you?
- Bonus points if you attach one emotion. Nike = "just do it" / hard work; New Balance = style ("made for the dad in Ohio and the supermodel in London").
- "can someone give you a title and can they add just one emotion to it?" [1:36:05]
- His own counter-position: "content strategist" with a data-science / analytics emotion, versus "here are my five favorite hooks" creators.
- He defers to Caleb's 6-hour branding video (transcript: "Caleb Braston"; *(inferred)* likely Caleb Ralston).

**F16. Virality recipe: TAM + storytelling + conversation (+ unique + hook)** [1:36:52–1:43:50] (Briar)
1. **High TAM (total addressable market):**
   - Widen the audience the video speaks to: "ladies" → "college girls" → "college girls that love Pilates" shows the TAM shrinking.
   - For sales: sales → business → money → ambition.
   - "most people just need to go one layer higher." [1:38:58] For top-of-funnel he's "trying to go for billions".
2. **Storytelling:** open curiosity at the start, open more loops as you go, use illustrations and metaphors, statistics, and be unique and specific.
3. **Conversation ("metrics category"):** decide whether the video is for **comments**, **sends** (group chats; "communal language", e.g. "me and the boys after we ___") or **saves**. "coming to a video, you can't usually hit both" [1:40:19], so pick one or two.
- Then: "We have to be unique… And then have a great hook, which those two things are harder to teach." [1:43:17]

**F17. Comment farm** [1:40:50–1:42:44] (Briar)
- Plant something odd in the frame that people will comment on. "something where we plant what I call a comment farm" [1:40:50]
- **His examples:**
  - Wall Street Wardrobe walks NYC with an open Birkin bag ("close the bag").
  - A laptop tab visible at the start of a clip.
  - Ashton Hall's ~40 one-second clips, with a bag left on the roof of his G-Wagon.
- Or ride a **hot topic** and connect it to your niche: Kylie Jenner / Forbes, Justin Herbert skipping an interview.

**F18. Hook + curiosity-loop scripting (live workshop)** [1:43:50–1:51:01] (Briar writes it, Tristan prompts)
- **Hook philosophy:** "I don't like thinking about the hooks too much. Like let's just say something crazy and then just get into it" [1:44:54]
- **Re-open a loop at about 15 s:** viewers who "got the point 15 seconds in" leave, so "reopen a new curiosity loop probably like 15 seconds in" [1:43:50]
- **The live build, step by step:**
  1. Strong statement (big cities: "you actually do not care about hard work")
  2. One-sentence reason why, "so we don't piss people off to the point where they're like this is stupid"
  3. Re-hook stronger ("If you had a gun to my head… $100,000 a month… Omaha, Des Moines, Fargo. **Why?**")
  4. "Why?" opens the next loop
  5. Payoff (nothing to do but work; cheap, so you reinvest)
  6. A wild closing line as a comment driver
- He cut one line on the spot ("I don't like that. Never mind.") [1:47:49]. Tristan keeps the "answering with a hook" moments in the episode on purpose. [2:07:25]
- When giving the hook, Briar steers away from "business owners" because it narrows the TAM. [1:45:43]

**F19. Viral For You (VFY)** [1:48:52–1:49:24] (Briar)
- "Viral" is relative: **about 4–5× your own baseline**. "I call it viral for you. VFY" [1:49:07]
- Example: 500-view baseline → 2,000 is viral for you. Then the baseline climbs (1,000 → 5,000, and so on).
- He expects the live-written Omaha video to do about 2× his 5–10K baseline (25–30K).

**F20. Weekly content pie (4 : 2 : 1 + stories)** [1:51:47–1:59:17] (Briar; "sneak peek inside" named by Tristan)
- **4 top-of-funnel (high TAM) posts.**
  - They must keep **a through-line** to what you sell: "It has to have a through line." [1:52:05]
  - Counter-example: a real-estate teacher posting about the Sydney Sweeney jeans campaign.
  - His Airbnb-coach examples: 5 cities no one's talking about; how much 5 Airbnbs made; 9 travel hacks; a celebrity's Airbnb.
- **2 middle-of-funnel posts.** "The main goal at the middle of funnel is to blow their mind." [1:54:30]
  - Blueprints with screens and software shown, e.g. an AI concierge that upsells stays.
  - "don't make stuff up" (his complaint about "what I learned as a TikTok intern" posts).
- **1 bottom-of-funnel post.** Show what you sell without pitching.
  - A client journey framed as "how [person] grew to X", repeating "on our next consulting call we did…".
  - Client wins.
  - A Q&A from inside your community.
  - Tristan names this copywriting principle "the sneak peek inside". [1:56:57]
- **Stories:** sell hard twice a week; the rest is life (runs, food, quotes).
  - "Stories are not a discovery mechanism." [1:58:30] They nurture and build affinity, so the "bait" there can't attract the wrong audience.

**F21. Fast-growing creators: two habits** [2:03:24–2:07:25] (Briar)
1. **They analyse data and stay on a linear course for a year or more.** He spends half his time with one client keeping her from detours ("randomly talk about like Art Basel in Miami"). [2:03:56]
2. **They don't miss days.**
