### 1. The four new hooks

#### Hook 1 · Expertise hook: "[N years] of [niche] in [60 seconds]"
- **Template (spoken):** "3 years of niche in 60 seconds." [0:02–0:04] · on screen: "3 years of **[niche]** in…"
- **Structure:** big time investment (years) + the topic + a tiny time cost (seconds). It's a compression promise: *my years, your minute.*
- **Why it works:** the ratio is the hook. "3 years" says you have authority without saying "I'm an expert"; "60 seconds" says the cost of watching is almost nothing. The viewer gets context (the topic) and a lean (benefit: skip years of pain) in one line, which is L01's "context + lean" in 6 words. *(our take)* It also sets an implicit contract: the viewer will stay to collect the condensed lessons, so it suits fast "blitz" bodies.
- **Reel's example:** **"3 years of therapy in 60 seconds."** [0:04–0:06] (a bearded man filmed close in the dark; the clip's own top text reads "3 years of Therapy in 1 minute"; pill: **40.5M** views).

#### Hook 2 · Warning hook: "Never start a [X] if you don't have these [Y] things"
- **Template:** "Never start an X if you don't have these Y things." [0:06–0:09] · on screen: "Never start a **[X]** if you don't have these **[y]** things"
- **Structure:** prohibition ("Never") + the big action the viewer is considering + a gate ("if you don't have") + a counted list ("these 4 things").
- **Why it works:** loss aversion: it protects the viewer from an expensive mistake before they make it. The number opens a loop (what are the 4 things? do I have them?), and the viewer self-checks each item. It's the "don't buy X" contrarian take turned into a checklist, so it ends with something useful, not just a debunk *(our take; Rahul's pure debunks were his weakest, see contrarian-take)*.
- **Reel's example:** **"Never start a business if you don't have these 4 things."** [0:09–0:12] (a woman in a yellow coat, word-by-word white captions, four green number tiles 1-2-3-4 pop in, then a big red "4 THINGS"; pill: **339K**).

#### Hook 3 · Desire hook: "This is exactly what you want to see when you're [X]"
- **Template:** "This is exactly what you want to see when you're X." [0:27–0:29] · on screen: "This is exactly what you wanna see when your **[x]**"
- **Structure:** a demonstrative ("This") pointing at a visual + "exactly what you want to see" (the ideal outcome) + a situation the viewer is in or will be in ("when you're buying…").
- **Why it works:** it shows *what good looks like* and lets the right viewer self-select: anyone in that situation feels addressed by name. The line only works with a visual: "this" must be on screen from frame 1, because the spoken context arrives at the end of the sentence. The visual is the proof, the words are the frame *(our take)*.
- **Reel's example:** **"This is exactly what you want to see when you're buying an industrial prop[erty]."** [0:29–0:32] (split screen: an aerial map shot of a warehouse with a red pin on top, the creator at his desk below; yellow bold captions with a dark stroke; pill: **3.6M**).

#### Hook 4 · Promise hook: "If you [do B] every single day, you're going to [X], [Y] and [Z]"
- **Template:** "If you take B every single day, you're going to X, Y, and Z." [0:33–0:35] · on screen: "If you take **[B]** every single day you gonna **[X],[Y] and [Z]**"
- **Structure:** a small daily habit (conditional) + "every single day" (consistency) + **three** concrete outcomes.
- **Why it works:** the cost is tiny and repeatable, the payoff is stacked three times, and the rule of three makes it feel complete and rhythmic. Each outcome is a separate reason to care, so it catches more viewers than one benefit would *(our take)*. It's the "pain or benefit" lean made into a whole first sentence.
- **Reel's example:** **"If you take creatine every single day, you're going to build more muscle, recover faster, and get way more out of all the hard work that you're putting in in the gym."** [0:36–0:43] (a man scooping creatine into a row of shot glasses, title "1. Creatine", small white word-by-word captions; pill: **28M**, the biggest example in the reel, and a gym one).
