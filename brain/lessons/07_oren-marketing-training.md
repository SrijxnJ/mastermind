## 2. Frameworks, models, checklists and rules (his terms)

### 2.1 Distribution is king [0:00]
- AI did for software what Alibaba and the internet did for physical goods. Building is no longer the bottleneck.
- > "In a world where you can build anything, distribution is king." [0:00]
- > "The barriers are now taste, distribution, and attention." [0:15]

### 2.2 The four types of marketing (+ ops) [2:36]
1. **Brand marketing:** perception, "untracked attention", positioning, feel and purpose. Examples: Patagonia, Rhode *(inferred from "road")*, Ramp's stunts.
2. **Product marketing:** what makes the product special and how to communicate it (messaging, presentation, packaging, roadmap). Examples: Graza ("sizzle and drizzle"), De Beers.
3. **Content marketing:** social plus collateral, decks and sales assets. Examples: Merit Beauty, Red Bull.
4. **Demand gen:** buying media and placing ads. Examples: HexClad, Comfrt, Grüns, supplement brands, TikTok Shop / Meta brands.
5. **Marketing ops** (the extra one): the systems and processes, e.g. Shopify back end, LLM projects.
- **Shift [5:01]:** the CMO used to orchestrate all of these. Now roles blend and younger leads are also individual contributors. "Content marketing and demand gen are becoming one."

### 2.3 Positioning ("lore") [5:32]
- **Definition:** what you're known for; the association you hold; the lens through which the brand sees the world and the world sees the brand.
- **Rules:** it cannot be generic. The #1 goal is to be remembered. It must be **AI-legible**, because ChatGPT and Google's AI answers "need to have a reason to show you a brand" [6:22].
  - > "positioning is simply what you're known for." [5:50]
  - > "The number one goal here is to be remembered." [6:06]
- **The shelf test [6:37]:** stand at the shelf (his example is energy drinks). Monster = counterculture, Alani Nu = female-centric, Celsius = health, Red Bull = content. Each has a reason to exist on the shelf. "What is your position?" applies to apps, agencies and services too.
- **Positioning catalogue [7:41–14:47]:**

| Type | His definition | His examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance** | Quantifiably better than what existed. Repeat the story, use metaphors, prove it in tests. "The best positioning you can have, but most brands don't have it" | The Absorption Company, David protein (the cod comparison) |
| **Category creation** | A known thing moved into a new form factor | Oura Ring *(inferred from "aura ring")* |
| **Counter-positioning** | A better experience vs a big incumbent for a certain group. "Our job is just to take market share" | Grüns vs AG1 (gummies, start with kids), Apple "I'm a Mac", Poppi / Olipop vs Coke |
| **Smart** | Add tech to a "dumb" category | Eight Sleep, smartphones, Nest |
| **Value** | Sell on cost. Weaker now because of Chinese supply chains (Temu, Shein). Still strong for services, local businesses and new agencies | — |
| **Niche** | "For us by us": language, background, body type, subculture. Buying becomes "an act of support", but it can limit TAM | Brandy Melville |
| **Generational** | The same experience, with messaging aimed at one generation. Purely a marketing choice | Jones Road (older), Half Magic (Gen Z) |
| **Single attribute** | Own one word in the consumer's mind | Volvo = safety |
| **Premium: true performance** | Products for people who take the activity super seriously; aspirational buyers follow | Satisfy Running |
| **Premium: lifestyle performance** | Bought by many who don't need it; needs real credibility | Arc'teryx, Salomon |
| **Premium: designer** | Aesthetics as the differentiator | Teenage Engineering |
| **Premium: bespoke** | One-of-one, custom. Harder to execute; carries a premium | — |
| **Counter-position to luxury** | "Luxury quality without the markup" | Italic, Quince *(inferred)* |
| **Name / oddball** | Borrow an existing audience through a name | "Satoshi Nakamoto" clothing brand |

- **Rule:** you need one of these; it is the **north star** for every ad, organic post, landing page, comms strategy and email. If the company lacks one, "that is your job to help bring that out" [15:19].
  - > "This is your north star." [15:03]
  - > "You build that strong position, everything else gets easier, your ads" [15:19]
- **Tool:** a "positioning tool" chat in the marketing.zip [14:16].

### 2.4 Brand world mapping (whiteboard exercise) [15:36]
For a new marketing role, a strategy reset or a new freelance client. His agency once sold it as a paid workshop, credited against the retainer.
1. **Characters:** everyone who could represent the brand: founder on camera, experts (e.g. Mango's designers), social team or hired creator, customers who make content (found with Outer Signal), collaborators, execs with podcasts.
2. **Sets:**
   - **Evergreen:** warehouse ("pack an order with us"), factory (he bought Osmos *(DJI Osmo, inferred)* for factory teams and hired local videographers through Upwork), an office corner made aesthetic, a monthly cyc-wall *(inferred from "psych wall")* studio day.
   - **Temporary:** e.g. the trade-show booth the night before the show.
3. **Events:** a calendar by quarter or month, with three kinds:
   - **Company:** launches, offsites, trade shows. Software teams can bundle 6–8 features into one release "mini event".
   - **Culture:** New Year, Coachella, summer, back to school, Valentine's, Black Friday. Ask ChatGPT what the target consumer pays attention to.
   - **Industry:** trade shows, competitor launches (WWDC, CES). Decide whether to launch head-to-head, before, after or at a different time.
4. **Plot:** **events, enemies, friends, specials**. "Think of this like TV." [20:50]
   - Enemies = competitor moves you lead, support or counter-position against.
   - Friends = partners and influencers' plans you can plug into or piggyback.
   - Specials = your own event or series to fill an empty stretch (Away's 7-episode late-summer social show ahead of Q3/Q4).
   - > "basically plot, we have events, we have enemies, we have friends, and we have specials." [20:35]
5. **Find the holes:** nobody willing to be on camera → hire someone. Content below standard → turn an area into a set. An empty quarter → create specials. **Refresh monthly or quarterly**. The calendar feeds email and social planning.
- **Career use [23:44]:** bring a mapped brand world (or a 60–90-day plan) to job interviews. A template is in the zip.

### 2.5 Messaging [24:31]: one statement, one style
- Mission, vision and tagline documents "really doesn't matter" in the modern age. What matters is a core position plus the ability to write it (with LLMs) for scripts, descriptions and emails.
  - > "I think you need one statement and one style." [25:34]
- **Positioning statement formula** [25:34–27:12]. He calls it a spin-off of Geoffrey Moore's framework *(spelling inferred from "Joffrey Moore")*:
  > **For** [target] **who** [need], [Brand] **is the** [category] **that** [benefit]. **Unlike** [competitor], **we** [differentiator].
  - His example: distance runners with side pain → an electrolyte supplement that "solves it permanently in seconds"; unlike sugary electrolyte drinks, "instant relief with no crash".
  - Order of thinking: target first, then their need (it can be cerebral: look cool, attract someone, make friends), then category and benefit, then alternatives, then why you're different.
- **Four copy styles** [27:27–30:53]. You can have more than one and "test your way into it":

| Style | Traits | Reference writer / brand |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct response** | Urgent, confrontational, numbers-driven, exact timelines, no ifs or buts. He calls it the style most marketers shy away from but the highest-efficacy one | Dan Kennedy *(inferred from "Diane Kennedy … his books")*. Brand: Hims ("Finish this sentence. The worst part of going bald is blank.") |
| **Evidence-led** | Methodical, testimonials, users' results, calmer and explanatory | Claude Hopkins |
| **Human-driven / story-driven** | Stories in everything, casual language, building in public | John Caples *(inferred from "John Capables")*. Brand: "Bad Ombres" frozen burritos (transcript spelling) |
| **Culturally savvy** (bonus 4th) | Pop-culture wordplay; only works if the brand, its people and its customers are culturally fluent | Reformation (email subject lines) |

  - > "by far the highest efficacy" [28:00] (direct response)
- **Advice:** read the books of the writer whose style you like. He includes a "copy styles" skill that rewrites any copy into evidence-led, direct-response and human-led versions [35:06].

### 2.6 The brand project in ChatGPT [31:25]
- A shared project per brand. **What to upload:**
  - the positioning statement
  - product sheets and product landing-page info
  - website copy
  - testimonials and review exports
- **What to leave out:** "I wouldn't even give it big brand guides".
- Give feedback ("I like this, not this"); the project remembers and the team can use it.
- **Then add the human layer** on top of the output: human case studies, real company moments, funny nuances. "That's what separates doing this mediocre versus doing it like all the big brands are doing it now." [33:00]

### 2.7 Five levels of AI adoption in marketing [33:47]
1. **Chatting:** "90% of teams" are here.
2. **Projects:** context applied to the intelligence.
3. **Skills:** "repeatable things become skills" [35:06], e.g. a quiz-maker skill or a category-comparison-ad skill.
4. **Agents:** e.g. his weekly Meta-ads summary agent with read permission.
5. **A "true brain for your marketing"** [36:57]: a central repository connected to ads, creative strategy, costs and BOMs that makes business decisions with you. Example: "Runith" from Motion (transcript spelling).
- **Single-player → multiplayer:** one named owner per project or skill keeps it updated.
  - > "moving from single player to multiplayer for AI." [35:22]
- His advice to individuals: master levels 2–3 (maintaining projects and making skills); "That is a skill that is worth money." [37:45].

### 2.8 Demand: three core ways + the "alpha" [38:17]
- A marketing team's job is to (1) define what differentiates, (2) map the toolkit, then (3) generate attention that converts.
- **Three ways:** organic social, advertising, outbound sales.
- **The alpha (advantage):**
  - > "social media gives you free attention." [40:07]
  - Still underused; competitors are "afraid to do it or don't know how".
- **Career rule:** don't fear analytics, ads or "putting yourself close to the money" [41:27].

### 2.9 Organic social: four ingredients + network guide [41:58]
- **Ingredients:** **narrative** (the story), **taste** (how it looks), **focus** (getting good at a few networks rather than "mid at all of them"), **credibility** (do people believe you).
- **TikTok:**
  - Gives attention based on video efficacy, but a branded business account "is going to have a hard time".
  - Winning means "not just to build an account, but to build an army" [42:30]: multiple accounts, creators, slideshows.
  - Without an in-house TikTok expert, "probably not worth pursuing beyond cross-posting" from Instagram.
- **Instagram:**
  - Gives views from the start, and "at any point in time, you can change your account's trajectory by simply posting better content." [43:33]
  - Product photos alone don't work; you need a strategy.
  - **Primary network for most brands**, reusable on TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook and X.
- **YouTube:**
  - For services businesses; a lead magnet.
  - 1–2K views can still sustain a business with quality leads.
  - Needs someone who cares about thumbnails and titles.
- **LinkedIn:**
  - > "LinkedIn is a team sport" [47:29]
  - Air *(inferred)* has 5–10 staff posting.
  - Buffer has 80%+ of employees active with bounty programmes.
  - The algorithm boosts intra-company interaction.
- **Three Instagram strategies** [44:04]. 90% of brands should pick one, mostly the first:
  1. **Expert:** one person on camera talking about the business's world.
     - > "This is the number one most effective and easiest way to promote your business on Instagram." [44:04]
     - Examples: Olivia for Opal *(inferred)* (separate handle, whiteboard videos); Ken Sakata / Front Office (general fashion deep dives).
     - Cadence: > "That is a three times a week kind of minimum proposition" [45:07]
  2. **Storytelling:** hero's-journey content about an interesting business, e.g. Bad Ombres ("we quit our jobs to take over this old Taco Bell"). About 2 posts a week (vlog + carousel). The business itself must be interesting.
  3. **Extremely art-directed:** a singular creative talent; conceptual, time-consuming. Example: Meshki *(inferred from "Meshkkey")*.

### 2.10 Goals, metrics and the content funnel [49:20]
- **Goals:** brand awareness and sales.
- **Per-content metrics:** **hook rate** ("whether people chose to watch your video"), views, saves, shares.
- **Account-level metrics:** total reach per month (awareness), leads / sign-ups / email adds, and sales attributed to social.
- **Content funnel** [50:22]:
  - **Top of funnel:** gets attention and may not mention the product. It raises the floor of all your content.
    - > "It's there to raise the floor of all your content." [50:55]
  - **Middle:** educates about the brand. Core content, usually fewer views.
  - **Bottom:** exists only to sell or get sign-ups; often low views.
  - His fashion example: viral stunts → fit/styling breakdowns → founder's "my 4–5 favourite pieces, get them now".

### 2.11 Context principles for organic plans [52:13]
- **The world matters more than the brand account:** experts, founders, creators and influencers drive more impact than the brand handle. "People gravitate towards personalities."
  - > "the world is more important than the brand accounts." [52:13]
- **You compete with the feed, not competitors:**
  - > "You are not competing with them." [53:15]
  - **ICP feed exercise:** make an account that follows what your ideal customer follows (find them via Outer Signal or by googling customers). Check it 10–15 min a day for a couple of weeks. The competition is "individual people … skits … hot guys and hot girls … funny and relatable content".
- **Shareability is the most important factor:** relatable experiences, expertise / value, tutorials, funny, interesting, aesthetically beautiful. Then measure whether it gets shared.
  - > "it has to be sharable." [54:20]
- **Examples:**
  - Kiyoko Beauty: founder on camera doing rankings and comparisons.
  - Crease Group: a hired creator doing "quest content", with a full TOFU→BOFU funnel and "how to work with us" videos.
  - Gel Blaster's regional TikToks: POV hands-only skits with voiceover; the Mexico account "blew up" to 800,000.
  - Blae's web-design studio: carousels plus green-screen brand-website teardowns.
  - > "you don't necessarily have to have someone on camera, but you have to have a concept." [57:14]

### 2.12 The pillar system: Level 1 and Level 2 of an organic plan [58:02]
- **Minimum team:** a social media manager plus someone on camera (ideally two people).
- **Formats = pillars:**
  - > "every business should have, in my opinion, between three and five formats you're testing." [58:35]
  - Examples: carousels, vlogs, sit-down explainers, green screen.
  - Run each 3–4 times a month, so 12–16 posts a month.
- **Monthly review** ("I wouldn't be overdoing your analytics"):
  - A couple of days after month end, compare views, saves, shares, hook rate, and new vs old followers.
  - Compare pillar vs pillar and month over month.
  - Report to stakeholders (top performers, what's improving, the changes you'll make).
  - About **1 hour a month**.
- **Validate or invalidate ideas:** a boss's idea becomes a pillar for a month. If it does worse **two months in a row**, swap it out.
  - > "They validate it with numbers." [1:00:25]
- **Cross-post everything** (YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, TikTok, X): "it compounds over time".
- **Level 2:** only once you're good at one network, build separate per-network strategies (e.g. TikTok = "more personal, a bit more candid. We're going to shoot it on iPhone" [1:01:44]).
- **Timeline (his claim):** traction in 3–5 months; improving into good content by 6 months at the latest.
- **Collaborator pillar:** a regular creator making about 4 collab posts a month brings in a new audience (he started as Dark Room's monthly collaborator) [1:02:16].

### 2.13 Idea and reference tools: hook anatomy [1:02:47]
- **Content Oracle** (his free GPT): describe the business, who buys and who can be on camera, and it generates ideas from his principles. Example output for an SF personal-finance accountant: "The 300k salary trap", "Your equity is not cash", "The SF rent test".
- **Social Growth Engineers:** paid case studies of fringe winners in niches like finance, dating and religion (he notes he has no relationship with them).
- **Video Database** (Cut30's app, built on Codex):
  - Browse by format (one-shot, interview Q&A, single photo, street interviews), topic and concept, and sort by **outliers** (≥2×).
  - For each video it gives a **hook analysis**, the narrative framework, the transcript and a **simple outline**.
  - > "What was their title hook, their verbal hook, and their visual hook?" [1:06:12]
  - > "It's a controversial question, rapid fire opinions, justifications, punch line." [1:06:28] (an outline he uses "all the time")
  - > "I'd highly recommend looking outside your niche." [1:05:41]
- **Archetypes cheat sheet:** brand styles as templates (in the zip) [1:07:15].

### 2.14 Ad terms and unit economics [1:07:15]
- **ROAS** (return on ad spend):
  - Worked example: 4× markup (fashion), $100 price, $25 cost. At 2× ROAS the ad costs $50, so the brand makes $25 [1:08:34–1:09:07].
  - "That is the math that basically makes all of online advertising go round and round."
- **CPA** = cost per action. **CAC** = customer acquisition cost. **LTV** = lifetime value.
- **First-order profitability** (cash-constrained early brands) vs judging CAC against LTV (later, with data).
- **3:1 LTV:CAC** as a north star: e.g. LTV $100 → target CAC $33. He credits "the common thread guys" with popularising it.
- **Ad-level metrics:** CTR, add to carts, checkouts initiated, pop-up email sign-ups, CPC, CPM, **thumb stop (= hook rate)**.
  - > "So CPM, CPC, thumb stop, those don't really matter in my opinion." [1:12:36]
  - Thumb stop is "a great indicator", but if the ad hits ROAS a bad thumb stop doesn't matter.
- **"Justifiable marketing":** the more quantifiable, the more budget.

### 2.15 Ad platform order [1:12:52]
1. **Meta** (Facebook + Instagram): "a money printing machine"; the #1 focus for 90% of brands.
2. **TikTok ads:** repurpose Meta ads. In some categories about 30%, sometimes 50%, of Meta's scale.
3. **YouTube ads:** horizontal versions. "Kind of underpriced"; a great second to Meta.
4. **Google search / display:** where there's search demand. A hard interface; connect via Windsor and similar tools.
5. **LinkedIn ads:** expensive; for B2B or high ticket, "far after Meta".
6. **Incremental** (at six to seven figures a month of spend): AppLovin (in mobile games), Shop app, Snapchat, each about 3–7% of spend. TV (e.g. Tatari) is out of scope.
7. **Newsletter sponsorships:** "underrated". Use the Beehiiv ad network, or ask ChatGPT for popular newsletters in your niche and their rate cards.

### 2.16 Meta ads: setup and the weekly loop [1:16:50]
- **Definition:**
  - > "media buying is simply directing money to things that are working." [1:17:23]
  - Plus continually testing new things, every week.
- **Biggest needle movers:** the customer and the right message (positioning), the product chosen, the **price point** (his fashion brand's sweet spot is $180–200 up to about $350), the **offer** (angle / deal / bundle), and the **creative** (designs, videos, hooks).
- **Setup:**
  - Auction → **Sales** objective (sometimes Leads).
  - **ABO (ad-set budget) for most starters**; CBO (campaign budget, "how much trust you have in Meta") only once you have lots of data.
  - **One ad set = one concept** (e.g. "founder ads", "art-directed carousels") with variations inside. Budget sharing off.
  - Conversions from everyone (not only new customers) to start; highest-volume bid; standard attribution.
  - **$50/day per ad set** ($1,500/month). $10 works but is slow; $100 is better.
- **Audiences:** lookalike from a customer list (**start at 3%, expand to 10%**) → interest targeting if there's no data → broad once Meta has data. "I would 100% start on lookalikes".
- **Weekly creative-strategy loop:** creative strategy → buy media → report → new creative strategy, "repeat forever".
  - Volume: big brands 50–100 (some 200–400) new ads a month. Cut30: 2–3 new ad sets a week × about 3 ads, so about 9–10 ads a week.
  - > "Three to 10 ads per set is a good rule." [1:24:51]
  - Variations should be "a completely different creative following the same theme", not light tweaks.
- **Testing rule:**
  - > "I'd be spending 3 to 5x what that target is" [1:25:23]
  - Example: target CPA $50 → $250 over 5 days.
    - 3 sales → keep testing.
    - 5+ sales → spend more ("scaling").
    - No sales but add-to-carts and checkouts → fix the landing page or message downstream.
    - Terrible → turn it off.

  - Use it for weekly read-outs and light changes (budgets, pausing).
  - Don't auto-publish ads, because "Meta will kind of shut accounts down that are automating it" [1:27:01].
  - Name ad sets by **customer profile + creative type** so the AI can reason over them, then ask it for hooks, angles and mock-up statics.

### 2.18 Characters: creators, influencers and the pyramid [1:30:58]
- **Creator** = up-and-coming, 0 to "a couple hundred thousand followers max", no celebrity status.
- **What creators are good for:**
  1. **Ad creative:** often the top performers in an ad account.
     - Needs **creative diversity** (different styles such as vlogs vs yapping, different looks).
     - > "It's a concept worth knowing called creative diversity." [1:32:35]
  2. **Seeding:** free product with no ask (suits low-cost items, $10–40 cost).
  3. **Affiliates:** paid on commission (TikTok Shop, ShopMy, LTK).
  4. **On-account or separate-account content:** e.g. Yah at Crease Group; Gymshark; Opal's whiteboard creator; "BeFreed" *(inferred from "Beef Freed")* with dozens of creators making similar videos.
- **Sourcing:**
  - Your own customer list first (Outer Signal; "a layup win").
  - Tribe (creators who make ads, paid on a percentage of spend), Sideshift, agencies (Dark Room), Later (managed services).
  - Meta's creator search, Social Snowball.
  - Creator search, vetting and first outreach make "a great use case" for building your own ChatGPT agent.
- **Influencer** = established, outsized effect; "can be a face or a voice". He calls himself one now (he helps debut Shopify Editions).
- **The pyramid** [1:36:01]:
  - **Micro / new creators** at the bottom: volume, experimentation, ad-account creative.
  - **Mid-tier** (about 30K–100K followers in a niche): "form the core".
  - A couple of **big influencers at the top as anchors**, who also get the creators below them excited.
  - > "And all three of those work in concert to make amazing marketing." [1:36:17]

### 2.19 The modular brief [1:37:21]
- A long checklist that you shrink or expand per campaign. Starred items are the easiest to drop.
- **The best 10% of creators don't need it; the bottom 90% do.** Let great creators "abandon this entirely", but don't let volume creators "just cook".
  - > "the best 10% of creators and" [1:37:52] … "they don't use the brief." [1:38:08]
- **Items:**
  1. **The two most important messages** (not 5–10).
  2. **Target demographic pitch:** a quick persona summary (ChatGPT can condense it).
  3. **Content type:** vlog / yap / static, and how flexible it is.
  4. **Hook inspiration**, split into **text on screen**, **visuals** and **what to say**. Reuse winning hooks across creators. Works well for **object hooks** (e.g. unboxing).
  5. **References:** high performers, Video Database bookmarks.
  6. **Mechanism** (starred), e.g. **"blitz"** ("10 out of 10 niche habits…", rapid-fire lists) or **storytime** (e.g. 7-minute TikToks).
  7. **Simple outline:** e.g. hook → scenario → product point 1 → joke → X.
  8. **Assets:** B-roll, graphics (especially for green screen).
  9. **Variance needed:** a comment-CTA version vs a separate CTA for the ad version, 2–3 hooks, alternate endings.

### 2.20 Whitelisting vs darklisting [1:41:18]
- **Whitelisting:** the creator's own post runs as an ad from their account via an **ad code** (Meta "partnership ad"). "They often perform way higher than regular ads"; some accounts run 30–50% of their ads whitelisted.
- **Darklisting:** an ad runs from the creator's account but was never posted to their feed (TikTok and Meta both allow it). "Somewhat less useful".

### 2.21 Tactics: the flow from attention to money [1:42:54]
- > "your goal as marketing is not to do cool campaigns." [1:43:09] The goal is leads and sales.
- **Attention sources:** ads, organic social, **referral**, **search** (Google and ChatGPT), foot traffic / shelf.
- **CTA:** every piece needs an ask (buy now, waitlist, download the cheat sheet, book a consultation).
- **Bio structure (3 lines)** [1:45:30]:
  1. What the company does.
  2. Why follow / what you'll learn.
  3. CTA (his own: "join my brand strategy newsletter").
  - > "you list what the actual company does." [1:45:30]
- **Landing page:**
  - A product page or a campaign-specific derivative (e.g. with the TikTok influencers' videos, or photos of a 50+ audience).
  - Use default pages at $50–100/day; build variants once you spend thousands.
  - **Conversion maths:** 100 visitors × $1 = $100. At 4% conversion that's 4 people, so $25 each; 11–12 conversions is far better, 1 is far worse.
- **CRM** (e.g. HubSpot; Klaviyo for email) holds leads vs customers.
- **Touches vs automation:** an abandoned-cart series is automation.
- **Enablement:**
  - **Speed to touch**, "a minute, 5 minutes". > "speed to touch is super crucial." [1:49:43]
  - **Thank-you page:** "extremely underrated" [1:50:31], e.g. a video from the person about to call you.
  - **Close assets:** most popular videos, testimonials ("50 examples of people that have a win with us"), FAQ / objections.
- **Weekly objection loop:** record calls (Granola), summarise the objections every week, and feed them back into content.
- **North-star metrics:** landing-page conversion rate and lead close rate (then qualified rate and lead volume).
  - > "obsess about the conversion rate here. This is your northstar metrics." [1:52:25]
- Map this funnel together with the sales team so "sales … love marketing".

### 2.22 Landing pages [1:53:26]
- **Purpose:**
  - > "So the entire role of this entire page is to remove any questions or objections that someone may have about buying the product." [1:54:45]
- **Two types:**
  - **Product page / PDP.**
  - **Listicle page** (content-heavy, BuzzFeed-style "14 steps…", which educates and then sells).
- **PDP formula he recommends for "90% of your PDP landing pages"** (from Van Group builds such as Rhode, Kai, HexClad, Ridge Wallet, Lola Blankets), in order:
  1. Lifestyle photo / video carousel.
  2. Buy the whole routine up top.
  3. Results on real people.
  4. Benefits reframed to the user ("what does it feel like?").
  5. Value props.
  6. How to use.
  7. Ingredients.
  8. Data / clinical / consumer results.
  9. FAQ for objections.
  - Variant: Ten Thousand shorts, with a short video per feature (liner, shell, pockets).
- **Luxury** (Jacob & Co.):
  - Answer "why is this worth this money?"
  - Design inspiration, animated sketches, development process, **macro craft shots**, then lifestyle and specs; CTA = inquire.
  - > "The job is basically to overwhelm you with how incredible it is." [2:00:22]
- **Habit:** whenever you click an ad, list the page's sections (e.g. AG1's comparison charts, goals, testimonials) as "recipes you can … test".
- **Three voices** for LP copy: direct response, evidence-led, story-driven. His generator outputs headline, sub-headline, problem, three proof points, objection handling, claims and offer in each voice.
- **Codex workflow:** a brand design **skill** (colours, backgrounds, buttons, imagery) → generate the page → critique it ("lighter… buttons… how does this look on mobile?") → deploy as a ChatGPT site or to your host.

### 2.23 Brand strategy vs creative strategy vs creative direction [2:03:47]
"These words are used interchangeably and they shouldn't be".
- **Brand strategy:** macro. Positioning, which messages to test, tone of voice, big bets (creator vs influencer, retail vs online) for a launch, quarter or year.
- **Creative strategy:** tactical, weekly, mostly the **ad account** (sometimes organic or creators). New hook angles and messages → briefs → distribution → feedback.
  - > "every single week coming up with new concepts to be put into ads." [2:05:07]
- **Creative direction:** "the holy grail". Drives the brand vision that enables creative strategy: campaigns, build-outs, packaging, merchandising. Can step in on video, photo, styling and design, and has a network to call on.
  - > "The creative director drives the brand vision that enables creative strategy." [2:06:27]

### 2.24 Campaigns and PR [2:08:02]
- **Campaign** = an idea in a moment in time that brings the whole brand world (founders, designers, ambassadors, email, partners, retailers, influencers) onto one theme.

- **Types:**
  - **Always-campaigning thematic feed:** Set Active (state fair → "out of office" Italy).

### 2.25 Email & SMS [2:13:34]

  - > "One a month is better than none." [2:27:30]
- **Campaign toolkit:**
  - **Shop the content:** reuse carousels and videos with more detail.
  - **New release.**
  - **From the community.**
  - **Business updates** ("Hey, Julie here…").

### 2.26 Sales support [2:28:02]
- > "the closer you are to the dollars, the more money you make." [2:29:22] (which is why creative strategists are paid well)

- **Making the most of leads:**
  - **Speed of response:** within 60 seconds vs two days later.
  - **Number of touches:** his high-ticket outdoors product (about $9K AOV) converted most at the **11th touch**.
  - **Triage:** qualified LinkedIn engagers go to a Slack channel for sales. Instagram followers in the service area get a DM ("Hey, saw you follow. Thinking about a new roof.").
- **Find "the hungriest salesperson"** rather than the best one.
- **Sales-call notes → ChatGPT** (Granola MCP, Fathom): ask what content would handle the objections and which pain points to reinforce.

## Takeaways (added to the Brain)
- [Brand & positioning] Positioning = "what you're known for": not generic, built to be remembered, and AI-legible (give ChatGPT / Google AI a reason to recommend you). [L07]
- [Brand & positioning] Shelf test: stand next to everyone else in the category and name your reason to exist on that shelf. [L07]
- [Brand & positioning] Positioning types to pick from: performance, category creation, counter, smart, value, niche, generational, single attribute, premium (true performance / lifestyle / designer / bespoke), counter-luxury, name. One becomes the north star for every post, ad and email. [L07]
- [Brand & positioning] One statement: "For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [competitor], we [differentiator]." [L07]
- [Brand & positioning] Brand world map: characters, sets (evergreen + temporary), events calendar (company / culture / industry) and plot (events, enemies, friends, specials); look for holes and refresh it monthly or quarterly. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Four copy styles: direct response (urgent, numbers, confrontational; his "highest efficacy"), evidence-led (testimonials, calm), human / story-driven, culturally savvy. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Three Instagram strategies: expert on camera (his #1; at least 3 posts/week), storytelling / hero's journey (about 2/week; the story must be interesting), art-directed. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] You compete with everything in your viewer's feed (people, skits, funny / relatable), not with niche rivals; study it with an account that follows what they follow, and look outside your niche. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Shareability test: what is the reason someone would send this to someone else (relatable, expertise, tutorial, funny, interesting, beautiful)? [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Content funnel: top = attention that "raises the floor", middle = educate about you, bottom = sell or sign up. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Break every reference into title hook + verbal hook + visual hook, its narrative framework and a simple outline (e.g. controversial question → rapid-fire opinions → justifications → punchline). [L07]
- [Content & hooks] No face is needed if there's a concept: POV hands-only skits with voiceover worked for Gel Blaster's regional TikToks. [L07]
- [Content & hooks] Content mechanisms: "blitz" rapid-fire lists (10 habits of X) and storytime. [L07]
- [Filming] Treat places as "sets": evergreen (office corner, studio day, warehouse) and temporary (a booth the night before an event). [L07]
- [Filming] For TikTok he makes content more personal and candid, shot on iPhone. [L07]
- [Growth] Pillar system: 3–5 formats, each 3–4×/month (12–16 posts); monthly review a couple of days after month end (views, saves, shares, hook rate, new vs old followers); swap a format after two worse months. [L07]
- [Growth] Get good at one primary network (usually Instagram) first, cross-post everywhere because it compounds, and add separate per-network strategies later. [L07]
- [Growth] Per-post metrics: hook rate, views, saves, shares. Account metrics: monthly reach, leads / sign-ups, attributed sales. [L07]
- [Growth] Collaborator pillar: a regular creator making about 4 collab posts a month brings in new audiences. [L07]
- [Growth] Bio in three lines: what you do / why follow / a CTA (his is a newsletter). [L07]
- [Growth] His timeline claim: traction in 3–5 months of a pillar system, good content by 6 months at the latest. [L07]
- [Business] Creator pyramid: micro creators for volume and ad creative, mid-tier (30K–100K) as the core, big anchors on top. Brands pay through seeding, affiliates, paid posts and whitelisting. [L07]
- [Business] Modular brief: 2 key messages, target, content type, hook inspiration (text / visual / spoken), references, mechanism, simple outline, assets, variants (CTA versions, 2–3 hooks). [L07]
- [Business] Whitelisting = a brand runs a creator's own post as an ad via an ad / partnership code; darklisting = an ad from the creator's account that never appears on their feed. [L07]
- [Business] Ad maths: ROAS, CPA, CAC, LTV, 3:1 LTV:CAC; test new ad sets at about $50/day with 3–10 distinct variations and spend 3–5× the target CPA before judging. [L07]
- [Business] "The closer you are to the dollars, the more money you make." Funnel = attention → CTA → landing page → lead / sale → CRM → fast touches, thank-you page, close assets. North stars: landing-page conversion and close rate. [L07]
- [Business] Email: campaigns vs automated flows, 1–3 sends a week, plain text from a person, and reuse posts and carousels as emails. [L07]
- [Business] Campaign map: teaser → campaign → unveil → sustain (→ refresh) across owned and external channels, with an asset list, owners, dates and a work-back. [L07]
- [Business] Weekly update up the chain: wins, what's next, blockers, KPIs. [L07]
- [Business] AI adoption levels: chat → project → skills → agents → "brain"; go from single-player to multiplayer, with an owner per project and skill. [L07]
