## 2. Every principle, test, rule and framework

### 2.1 The three rules (applied to every sentence)
- **Exact terms:** "Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say this?"
- **Scoring:** "you get three no's, you're probably writing a lot of rubbish. You get three yes's, you're onto something." [1:23–1:39]
- Bad example: recruitment ad "don't just get a job, change an entire industry" fails all three (can't see it, can't prove it, any recruiter could say it). [1:39–2:10]
- Good example: New Balance "Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio" passes all three: you can picture both people, it's true, and "it's bespoke to New Balance… something they can only say". [2:10–2:42]

**Rule 1: Can I visualize it? (concrete vs abstract)**
- > "If you can't visualize it, you won't remember it." [2:57]
- Quotes Lisa Cron: "if I can't see it, it's not there yet." [2:57]
- **Word-memory game:** "seamless transition, charging pitbull, muscular Irishman, better way, leg of lamb". David recalls Irishman, pitbull, leg of lamb. (Harry says "six words"; five phrases are read.) [3:14]
- **Definition of abstract:** "it's intangible. You can't drop it on your foot." [3:30–3:45]
- **Technique: "zoom in on the words".** On paper, draw a line down the left side, "abstract" at top, "concrete" at bottom; keep rewriting and asking "what do I actually mean here?" until you end on "a concrete object". [3:45–4:16]
- Juxtaposition adds humour (supermodels vs dads). [4:33–4:48]

**Rule 2: Can I falsify it? (can it be proved true or false)**
- Why it works: "as a writer you're putting your head on the chopping block… our ears prick up." [5:39–5:55]
- **Technique: "you can't talk, you can only point"** (from a deleted Reddit account). [6:29–6:45]
- Blind-date game: subjective words ("good family, great values, smart, funny") make the listener think "you're just saying stuff"; pointable facts ("Looks like Ryan Gosling, 6'2"", "Reads on the tube") persuade. [6:45–8:05]
- Pointing "gets you off the adjective trail". [8:38]
- > "This takes work. That's why it's better." [9:12]

**Rule 3: Can nobody else say this?**
- Quotes "Jim Durkey" (transcript spelling; *probably Jim Durfee: inferred*): "never write an ad a competitor can sign". [9:20–9:30]
- It "forces you to look a little bit deeper at what you're selling." [9:30]
- Example: the speedometer ad, "Your car has five numbers on the speedometer… [this car] has six. One could get the impression that the people who made your car lack a little confidence." The visual aligns with the text. (Transcript names both Volkswagen and Volvo here; which brand ran it is unclear.) [9:30–10:01]
- Slogan from the cold open/this section: **"Write copy that can't be copied."** [0:15, 28:05]

### 2.2 Why copywriting matters (the case Harry makes)
- **Same product, different words:** Snickers vs Fuse: "both bars look the same. They taste the same", but one is the world's best seller and one was discontinued. "Startups, businesses are like these two bars… strip away the copy… they do kind of the same thing." [10:18–10:50]
- Quotes Ogilvy: "we don't choose the whiskey, we choose the image." [11:06]
- **Positioning principle (Kitson story):** "what can I do that no one else is doing that people care about?" A modest footballer's tell-all book, published as "The Secret Footballer", outsold star players' books. "Just by positioning it, just by a little bit of storytelling, he could outsell him." [11:23–13:14]
- "It's the number one skill in marketing." [0:15, 25:42]

### 2.5 The writing process: point A to point B, then three pieces

- **Piece 1: Who you're talking to.**

- **Piece 2: Have something to say.**

  - His own: "every marketer should learn copywriting." Marketing Examples began because learning marketing was "two pages on theory… just give me examples." [25:42, 26:46]

- **Piece 3: Say it well.**
  - Hinge "The dating app designed to be deleted": alliteration ("DDD"), juxtaposition ("who makes an app that wants to be deleted?"), and an implied happy ending. "Now, Hinge owns that phrase." [27:18–28:21]

### 2.10 Facts
- > "If in doubt, give me a fact." [46:58]
- "When you say a fact, it… guarantees that you say something. Most people when they open their mouths, they say nothing. They say wallpaper, word-shaped air." [46:11]
- A raw fact may be dull ("350 million Americans"): "you got to work with a fact… start with a fact. It's true. Then make something with it. A fact doesn't have to be numeric." [45:56, 47:15]
- Heinz example: staff decanting non-Heinz ketchup into Heinz bottles → a possible ad line, "Even when it's not Heinz, it's Heinz." [47:15–47:46]
- Pundit example: not "they're just not playing well… Southgate this, Southgate that", but "they're playing Kieran Trippier at left-back when he's a right-back. That's a fact." [48:01–48:17]
- Quotes Orwell on modern writing not "picking out words for the sake of their meaning"; "You use a fact, it's precise." [49:07–49:24]
- "Inequality": don't say "a bit unequal" (an adjective); "Show me a graph." [49:24–49:39]

### 2.11 The newsletter method
- Ann Handley: "less about the news and it's more about the letter." [51:00]
- **Intro = time, place, what's going on in my life** (example: J.K. Rowling's *Chamber of Secrets* opening at breakfast in Privet Drive). Harry "look[s] outside the window". [51:15–51:49]
- **Three things he does:**
  1. **Density:** "you get five things in a newsletter six things… It's dense." [52:05]
  2. **Conflict in pretty much every example:** before/after, problem/solution, "here's how they do it, here's how they do it." "We as human beings… remember stuff relatively" (pickle juice vs orange juice). [52:05–52:38]
  3. **No paragraph longer than two lines since March 2021:** "short paragraphs are like monkey bars. They're easy to swing between"; a 3-line paragraph often means "I'm not explaining myself as well as I… could be"; and "giving lines room to breathe". [53:42–54:30]
- **Loom positioning (conflict in action):** other screen recorders said "easier to use screen recorder"; Loom said remote communication sucks, then introduced a new category, "async video messaging". [52:38–53:26]
- **Fresh-bread intros:** the "3:47 a.m." intro (laptop, green tea, whiteboard, "Is this any good? I don't know.") should "feel like it's out the oven… warm to touch". Noticing what's in front of you is the material; "it's not particularly clever… it's actually boring." [54:30–56:05]

### 2.12 Writing simply = rewriting
- > "You can't write simply. You can only rewrite simply really." [56:23–56:40]
- **Kaplan's Law of Words** (spelling in transcript varies: Kaplan/Caplin): "any words that aren't working for you are working against you." **Harry's Law of Words:** "you aren't taking Kaplan's Law of Words seriously enough." [56:56–57:11]
- **Applies to ideas too:** "the strength of an idea is inversely proportional to its scope." "the word and on a landing page is normally never a good thing." Hiut Denim: "We make jeans, that's it. Do one thing well." [57:11–57:43]
- Album covers as single-idea examples: Velvet Underground banana, Dark Side of the Moon prism, Bowie's *Aladdin Sane* lightning; Bowie's *Reality* as a bad cover ("seven disparate images"). [57:43–58:14]
- **Paragraph = burrito:** "I should be able to throw it to you and you should be able to catch it and it shouldn't come apart in the air." [58:14–58:30]
- **Pull-out test:** "If you could pull a sentence out and it still works, the sentence probably shouldn't have been in there in the first place." [58:45]
- **Write 4–5 versions side by side**, giving "freedom to do it wrong". [59:01–59:33]
- **Show versions for feedback:** one paragraph gets "I like it" / "I don't like it"; three versions get "That one's the best. Use that sentence, but put it in that one." [59:33–1:00:04]

### 2.13 Structure

### 2.14 Standards and knowing what good looks like

## Takeaways (added to the Brain)
- [Content & hooks] Run every line through Harry Dry's 3 rules: can I visualize it, can I falsify it, can nobody else say it (3 no's = rubbish, 3 yes's = onto something). [L09]
- [Content & hooks] Turn abstract into concrete by "zooming in on the words" (regain fitness → Couch to 5K; pretty people → supermodels in London). [L09]
- [Content & hooks] "You can't talk, you can only point": swap adjectives for things that can be proved true or false; "if in doubt, give me a fact". [L09]
- [Content & hooks] One Mississippi test: if the line isn't understood in about 2 seconds, it fails (though Harry also says not everyone has to get it). [L09]
- [Content & hooks] First line exists to get the second line read (via Dave Gerhardt / Joe Sugarman). [L09]
- [Content & hooks] Conflict everywhere: before/after, problem/solution, the 3 enemies (approach, belief, competitor), juxtaposition; in stories, build on "but" without writing it. [L09]
- [Content & hooks] Explain something new by comparing it to things people know ("tougher than an F-150, faster than a 911"). [L09]
- [Content & hooks] Make a small number big by expanding the time frame (22,000 career hours), and reframe a cost as a saving. [L09]
- [Content & hooks] Intro = time, place, what's going on right now; notice what's in front of you ("fresh bread, warm to touch"). [L09]
- [Text & fonts] Kaplan's Law of Words: any word not working for you is working against you; if a sentence can be pulled out and the paragraph still works, cut it. [L09]
- [Text & fonts] No paragraph longer than 2 lines ("monkey bars"); get key lines onto one line; bold the rhythm/repeated words. [L09]
- [Text & fonts] Metonymy: swap the literal word for a visual one ("1,000 songs in your pocket", not "media player"). [L09]
- [Text & fonts] Neil French: one element (header, subheader, picture or logo) can carry an ad; "it doesn't look like an ad, so you read it". [L09]
- [Text & fonts] Write and design in the final tool, and judge it in context next to competing content. [L09]
- [Brand & positioning] "Never write an ad a competitor can sign": say what only you can say ("write copy that can't be copied"). [L09]
- [Brand & positioning] Positioning: what do you replace, and what can you do that no one else is doing that people care about (Secret Footballer, Loom's "async video"). [L09]
- [Brand & positioning] Have something to say: build from a belief or something you think is wrong with the world. [L09]
- [Brand & positioning] Tone must fit the brand (Hinge isn't silly, so no mascot; Duolingo is). Make the customer feel smart. [L09]
- [Brand & positioning] One idea beats many: the strength of an idea is inversely proportional to its scope ("We make jeans, that's it"). [L09]
- [Growth] Know who you're talking to and what they're doing at that moment (St Ives café: "coffee and pastry = £5" beat the big menu). [L09]
- [Growth] Newsletter: less news, more letter; 5–6 dense examples with conflict in each; about 50% open rate on 100K+ subscribers (Harry's numbers). [L09]
- [Process] Seed idea + a reference layout → many rewrites (2 days, 25 rewrites) → spitball without judging (dirty tap water) → feedback from people with taste → a final design pass. [L09]
- [Process] Write 4–5 versions side by side; showing versions gets far better feedback than showing one. [L09]
- [Process] Structure = dividing lines + parallelism (first 100 readers / next 9,900). [L09]
- [Process] Standards: only 9/10+ goes out; it's done when nothing can be added or removed ("walk around the garden"). [L09]
- [Business] Copywriting as "the number one skill in marketing": 1% → 2% conversion doubles growth; same product, different words (Snickers vs Fuse). [L09]
- [Business] AI is a tool only as good as your taste; taste, conviction and first-hand experience are what it can't supply (Harry's view). [L09]
- [Habits] "Silence and action": a phone-first morning makes the day "drudge"; silent writing days and walks without earphones. [L09]
