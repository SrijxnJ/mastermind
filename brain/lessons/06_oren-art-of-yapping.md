# Lesson 06: The Art of Yapping (talking to camera)

- **Source:** "The Art of Yapping (full guide to talking on video)", **orenmeetsworld** (Oren John), youtube.com/watch?v=CS0A4hJfcy4, about 17 min 50 s. Sponsored by HubSpot.
- **Studied:** 27 Oct 2026. Full transcript plus a visual production breakdown, both via vidIQ (30 credits).
- **What "yapping" means:** putting an iPhone on a tripod and just talking to camera. He says it's the simplest form of being a creator.

## The lesson

### Why yap (his reasons)
- Short-form video is now "the primary method of business and personal communications". People share reels in group chats and work Slacks. [1:02–1:35]
- He started making content at about 37, and the biggest value was becoming **a better communicator**: "It's made me a better salesperson… a better marketer… better at articulating ideas." [1:50–2:38]
- Content makes you workshop **ideas** with people around you, which leads to "a more thoughtful life". [2:38–3:25]

### Types of yaps (formats)
| Type | What it is | Example he shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard yap** | Phone on, sitting, talking to camera. Can work even without captions, but he recommends captions "for almost everybody" | Brandon (TikTok); a creator reviewing the best chicken Caesar wrap in Orange County [4:45–5:17] |
| **Walking yap** | Talking while moving; "seems more like a FaceTime with a friend". Not for beginners ("too many variables") | Christian ("a yap king") [5:17–5:39] |
| **Car yap** | Candid talking from the car, not always looking at the lens | Tom (Cut30 grad) [5:39–5:53] |
| **Graphic yap: chaotic** | Overlay images or clips from the camera roll with Edits/CapCut's overlay function. **About one new visual every 2 s.** For a 60 s video, collect about 30 images | Cut30 grad [6:27–7:07] |
| **Graphic yap: framed** | Frame yourself low and leave space above your head for a graphic; plan head, caption and graphic placement. **Keep graphics vertical** | Cut30 grad [7:07–7:39] |
| **Graphic yap: full-screen** | Full-screen 9:16 images made in Canva, cut over the talking, about one every 2 s | Oren's own videos [7:39–8:10] |

> "The key to the yap is you just do it. The whole thing is set up to make you want it to be easy." [8:10]

### What to talk about: 5 frameworks [8:20–11:04]
1. **Strong take:** say something is the best or the worst, then defend it. "Conversation is what drives videos." *E.g. "Orlando is the best sports city in North America."*
2. **Strong take → education:** use the take as a hook, then pivot to teaching. *(His Coachella example: "too many Coachella influencers", then a breakdown of content supply and demand.)*
3. **Small epiphany:** a relatable everyday observation ("Do you notice how husbands always do this?"). It builds camaraderie.
4. **Humour:** something funny, or poking fun at something (e.g. a creator critiquing overrated places in LA).
5. **Story time:** a narrative from your life.

> "Strong takes, small epiphanies, and story time is like the foundation for 90% of the content you can make." [10:49]

**What he deliberately left out:** "my top three favourite…", "three ways to…", motivational speeches. "If you're thinking outside of that box and it's generic, it's not going to work." [10:49–11:04]

### Where ideas come from: the Yap Map [11:04–12:53]
Use dead time (a commute, where you'd normally scroll) to write in a notes app:
- **Touch points of your day:** work questions you had to explain, lunch conversations, jokes, shows people are talking about.
- **Personal life:** dating, interpersonal moments ("have you noticed guys always…").
- **Media you consume:** trends or news you can react to.
- **Shower/workout thoughts.**

Goal: **3–5 ideas a day** ("I have hundreds and hundreds now"). For each idea, find **a take**: a strong opinion, a story, or something relatable to break down.

### How to script it [12:53–14:43]
| Framework | Structure |
|---|---|
| **Hook → Story → Point 1 → Point 2** | Hook ("Kick Coffee is the best coffee shop in Newport Beach"), then a real anecdote, then your 2 reasons |
| **The "8 Mile"** | Hook, then **call out the opposite argument and disprove it**, then make your point. It removes objections up front and keeps people watching (a reference to the *8 Mile* rap battle) |
| **The 4 or 5 things** | "Observations I made at Coachella as a brand strategist": 4 mini videos, each a strong take; people stay for the next one |

> "You are there to light people up… be excited, be passionate, care, have fun with it… If [you look like you're not having fun], it's probably not going to do very well." [14:43–15:15]

### Recording [15:15–16:18]
- **iPhone + a mic:** Røde plug-in USB-C mic; wired Apple EarPods also work; "AirPods do not work".
- **The ReCut method:** pause and stay silent whenever you need to think, keep eye contact, then talk again. Remove the silences afterwards with the **ReCut** app (~$20) or an editor's silence-removal. A 6–7 min raw clip is fine.
- **Redo the start at the end:** your intro is the most important part, but it's where you're most awkward. After 3 minutes you're in flow, so re-record the intro then.
- **Edit in CapCut or Edits:** add captions and a **title**. He calls both "really important".
- **Mindset:** "People want to hear from people that are at their level or a level right above them… there is nobody who is too old or in too weird a niche to do this." [16:18–16:50]

## Production (editing, sound, animation, captions): from the visual breakdown
| Area | What he does |
|---|---|
| Hook (0–15 s) | Says the whole promise immediately ("…a complete playbook for talking into your phone to change your life") + an instant title card + rapid vertical-video examples |
| Pacing | Average shot about **2.5–3.5 s**; jump cuts remove breaths (the ReCut method in action); a visual change every 3–5 s |
| Zooms | Subtle **105–110% punch-ins** on key phrases and when each numbered framework starts |
| Layout | Single camera, medium-wide, centred; 9:16 examples in **rounded-corner windows on the left ~40%** with him visible on the right |
| Camera/light | Fast prime (~35–50 mm look) at wide aperture with a soft blurred background; soft key light from front-left; classic set (white moulding, gold-framed sailboat painting) |
| Sound | Continuous **lo-fi/chill-hop bed at about −22 to −24 dB** under the voice; **pops** on every graphic/logo/title; **whooshes** on window slide-ins and text wipes; **UI clicks** on screen recordings; an *8 Mile* movie clip as a sound gag; music rises at the outro |
| Voice | Close boom mic, compressed, de-essed, high-pass filtered: a clean "broadcast" tone |

## Takeaways (added to the Brain)
- [Content & hooks] 90% of content = strong takes, small epiphanies, story time; avoid generic lists, "3 ways to" and motivational speeches. [L06]
- [Content & hooks] Strong take → education: use the opinion as the hook, then teach. [L06]
- [Content & hooks] Scripts: Hook → Story → 2 points · "8 Mile" (state the counter-argument, disprove it, then make your point) · 4–5 mini takes. [L06]
- [Content & hooks] Yap Map: log 3–5 ideas a day from your day, media and shower/workout thoughts; give each one a take. [L06]
- [Filming] Yap formats: standard, walking, car, graphic. A tripod + Bluetooth remote + plug-in mic is enough; AirPods are not. [L06]
- [Filming] Pause in silence to think (ReCut method) and re-record the intro at the end, when you're warmed up. [L06]
- [Editing & pacing] Graphic yap: about 1 overlay every 2 s (~30 images per 60 s); frame yourself with headroom for vertical graphics. [L06]
- [Effects] 105–110% punch-in zooms on key phrases and new sections. [L06]
- [Sound] Lo-fi bed at about −22 to −24 dB under a compressed, de-essed voice; pop/whoosh/click on graphics. [L06]
- [Text & fonts] Yellow bold serif section titles + clean sans UI. [L06]
