## Structure: what the top third shares

- Base rate: 14.3% of Shorts and 13.0% of long videos beat their channel median by 3x. In the recent window it is 10%: newer videos have had less time to collect views, so a recent 3x is worth more than an old one.
- Practical test (28%), payoff tease (25%), head to head (21.5%), which-one question (19.7%) and first-person experiment (19.7%) are the only shapes clearly above the 14% base rate. Each of these promises a result the viewer can see.
- First-person openers ('I sent', 'I went', 'I built', 'my') hit 3x 19.4% of the time vs 14.0% without (124 titles).
- Titles with an ALL-CAPS word hit 11.1% vs 14.9% without. Shock words and emoji stacks have the lowest lift (0.83).
- A current-cycle topic in the title (iPhone Duo/18, BBD, RAM prices, 25H2, RX 9060 XT) hits 16.6% vs 13.8% without.
- Title length barely separates: median 9.0 words in the top third vs 8.0 in the bottom. 11+ word titles do 15% vs 13% for titles of 4 words or fewer.
- Language does not move the multiple: Devanagari titles 13.7% vs 14.2%, Hinglish in Roman script 13.2% vs 14.3%. Regional channels (Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada, Marathi, Bengali) follow the same formulas.
- A ₹ price in the title is 13.6% vs 14.3%. Everyone uses one, so it no longer stands out.
- hookscore.py rates the top and bottom thirds the same (median 42). It scores English spoken hooks and cannot read titles, so ignore it for this set.
- Series parts decay fast: PC Tech Talk's 'PC Build From Local Shop' went Part-1 0.95x, Part-2 81x, Part 3 3.8x, Part 4 1.3x. The hit came on part 2, so one flop doesn't kill a series, but the follow-ups ride the hit and then fade.
- Length: 19 dated outliers had a median Short length of 60 s; Rahul's Instagram reels ran 42-179 s. The length evidence is thin.

## What works

- Put the test in the title: boot time, battery drain, drop test, temperature, FPS. 24 of 85 practical-test videos beat 3x (28%) across 13 accounts. TechTerminus 'Macbook vs Windows Laptop ⚡️Boot Time Test' 65.8x; androwide 'Practical 5G Battery Drain Test' 89x; Beebom 'iPhone Duo Drop Test Gone Wrong!' 10.2x (Oct 15).
- Two things head to head (A vs B): 31 outliers from 20 accounts, twice as common in the top third as in the bottom.
- First-person field reports and experiments: Techbeast (18K subs, median 21K) did 'Will RAM Prices Go Down in 2027?' 37x, 'I Sent a Stranger to Buy a ₹1 Lakh Gaming PC' 26x and 'I Investigated RAM Prices in Nehru Place' 20x, all in the last ~6 weeks.
- The buyer's question as the title ('Should You Buy RTX 5060Ti?', 'kaun sa lein?'): 19.7% hit rate, 12 accounts.
- Local PC-shop stories: PC Tech Talk 'PC Build From Local Shop, दुकानदार कहाँ कहाँ बेवकूफ़ बनाते हैं?' 81x; Rahul's 'before you walk into the shop' reel; Gaming PC Wala shop skits up to 145x.
- Specific Windows one-command tips on small accounts: techoverpc 'turn on pc with keyboard' 33x (358 subs), TechnoBaazi 'Copy Windows to SSD' 16.6x, technouplift 'You are Not DELETING DATA Permanently' 12.4x.
- Price alarm while prices are rising: RAM/PC-price titles are the best formula in the 60-day window (5 of 23) and 27% for accounts under 100K.
- Small accounts can win: 643 videos from accounts under 100K beat 3x 14.9% of the time, the same rate as the giants. The multiple does not depend on size.

## What doesn't

- Shock words and emoji stacks (😱🔥🤯, INSANE, OMG): the lowest lift of any shape (0.83).
- ALL-CAPS words in the title: 11.1% vs 14.9%.
- Roast and expose titles: under-index in the top third and 1 outlier in 33 recent uses. Technical Dost and Gyan Therapy own this, and it is crowded.
- Buyer warnings ('don't buy', 'mistake', 'before you buy'): 1 outlier in 34 recent uses. The format is worn out.
- Sale-deal roundups (BBD, Great Indian Festival, Diwali): 180 recent videos, only 7.8% at 3x. Saturated.
- AI-tool tips: 61 recent videos, 2 outliers (3%). GTA 6 hype: 14 recent, 0 outliers.
- Plain product labels and numbered 'Top 5 under ₹X' lists in Shorts: both below baseline.
- Vague 'secret / hack / trick' titles with no concrete action: they pull the one-trick formula below baseline even though specific commands work.

## Method

ig-viral outlier method on Indian tech creators. YouTube is the corpus: one paced yt-dlp --flat-playlist request per channel (last 40 Shorts; the last 30 long videos only when a channel had under 8 Shorts), 2.5 s apart, no parallel requests, no login, no vidIQ. Outlier multiple = views / that channel's own median for the same format; 3x+ is signal. Hook = the video title (hook_source 'title'), cleaned of trailing hashtags and channel-name suffixes. Captions were not pulled: single-video requests hit YouTube's 'confirm you're not a bot' wall after 19 videos, so per-video fetching stopped there and was not worked around. Those 19 give real dates and lengths; other dates are estimated from list position times the channel's dated cadence (marked 'est:'), or left null. 'Recent' = dated or estimated within 60 days, or among a channel's 15 newest Shorts when the title carries no older year. Instagram: no profiles were opened or listed. The 12 @rahulmhinduja reels come from the existing brain study (brain/creators/rahulmhinduja.md, 27 Oct 2026); one public reel URL was re-read with yt-dlp, and Instagram returned no view count. Formulas: a tech-title classifier (T1-T15, each mapped to the nearest ig-reel hooks.json id), because hooks.json's English spoken-hook patterns leave 77% of these titles unclassified. swipe.py was also run as-is on captured.tsv (all 3x+ rows plus per-channel controls) and wrote tech_swipe.md. Copy the formula, never the video; every row is attributed.

## Confidence

Medium for the formula ranking, low for 'viral now' timing. 3697 videos across 100 YouTube channels plus 12 Instagram reels from one account. The formula differences are real but modest (14% base rate; the best formulas reach 20-28%), and each top formula has 3x+ hits from at least 12 accounts. Caveats: (1) hooks are titles, not spoken first lines, because captions were blocked; (2) only 19 videos have real dates, and other recency is estimated from list position, so older evergreen hits are mixed in; (3) the newest-first lists span about 6 weeks for daily posters and years for slow ones, so medians are 'recent 40', not 'recent 30 days'; (4) the classifier is regex on titles, so a row's formula is a label, not a reading of the video; (5) Instagram is a single account.
