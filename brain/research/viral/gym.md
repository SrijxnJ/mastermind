# Indian gym & fitness: what is actually beating its own baseline (Oct 2026)

Updated 2026-10-27. Companion data: `gym.json` (full) and `gym_swipe.md` (swipe.py ranking of all 1,451 gym titles).

## Sample and method

- **105 Indian creators** listed, **99 YouTube channels measured**, **3,722 videos** (latest 40 Shorts per channel, or 40 long videos if no Shorts tab), **1,451 gym-relevant**.
- **212 videos at 3x+** their own channel median, across **61 channels**.
- Ranked by **outlier multiple = views / that channel's median**, never raw views. Hook = video title minus hashtags (`hook_source: title`).
- One paced flat-list request per channel. 23 outliers got exact date/length before YouTube returned 429 and 'confirm you're not a bot'; I stopped there. No captions, no Instagram crawling, no login.
- Dates: latest 40 uploads per channel as of 27 Oct 2026; exact dates known for 23 outliers spanning 2021-04-06 to 2026-10-10; most active channels' windows cover roughly mid-2025 to Oct 2026.

## What the top third share (structure)

- Top third (multiple >= 1.44x) vs bottom third (<= 0.76x), 483 titles each, gym-relevant titles only.
- Hook length is not the lever: median 6 words up top vs 6 at the bottom (also 6 words among the 65 videos at 10x+).
- hookscore.py does not separate them (median 37.4 vs 39.2). Expected: its own docs say it cannot split a creator's hits from misses.
- Viewer-participation and contest words (who is stronger / guess / vs / beat me) are 5.0% of the top third vs 2.3% of the bottom; among 3x+ outliers 6.6% vs 2.4% of sub-0.76x videos.
- Emoji in the title: 38.1% top vs 33.3% bottom. Questions: 13.9% vs 12.0%. Numbers: 24.8% vs 23.6%. All small gaps.
- Language of the written title: top third {'english': 401, 'hinglish': 54, 'devanagari': 21, 'regional-script': 7} vs bottom {'english': 415, 'hinglish': 39, 'devanagari': 28, 'regional-script': 1}. Roman Hinglish titles slightly over-index (54 vs 39); Devanagari titles slightly under-index (21 vs 28). English titles dominate both, even on Hindi-speaking channels.
- Smaller channels beat their own median more often: 19.5% of videos from channels under 100K subs hit 3x+, vs 12% for channels over 100K. Median subs of top-third rows 210000 vs 285000.
- Big multiples are mostly older in each channel's 40-short window (median feed position 21 vs 14): YouTube Shorts keep accruing views for months, so a 3x on a video under 2 weeks old is a stronger signal than the same 3x on a 6-month-old one.
- What the top shares that titles cannot show (read from the outliers by hand): the payoff is visual and fast (a lift, a reveal, a stranger's reaction), the viewer is given a job (guess, judge, rate, beat me), and there is a second person on screen (opponent, mummy, stranger, kid). The bottom third is dominated by explainer titles from coaches ('How to...', 'Best...') and bare day-vlog titles.
- Length (only 23 videos have exact length, all Shorts): outliers ran 8 to 115 s; the 4 biggest recent ones were 28 s (ShredwithVK reveal), 69 s and 115 s (Kunal Lulaz contests), 89 s (Hyrox guide). Contests and guides can run long; reveals stay under 30 s.
- CTA: not visible from titles. Pattern seen in titles/descriptions: 'Share with your brother', 'Subscribe agar naye ho toh', 'Rate: Flop/Hit/Superhit/Blockbuster', 'Let me know your PRs': a single viewer job, not a follow ask.

## Works

- Give the viewer a job. Guess-the-PR / who-is-stronger / rate-my-transformation titles are 14 of the top third vs 1 of the bottom (6 accounts). Kunal Lulaz: 'Who is Stronger?' 9.9M at 198x (Jul 2026), 'Beat me in arm wrestling, win Rs 5000' 7.5M at 151x (May 2026); Fit Bros 'Random guessing deadlift PR' 1.9M at 88x.
- Quote the doubter, then answer (15 vs 4, 6 accounts): ShredwithVK 'Transformation aisi karo ki 4 log bole AI he' 133x; Prem Joshi 'Dabbe wali body banai hai tune?' 11x; Aadarsh 'They said you can't get abs without steroids' 7.7x.
- Transformation reveals sold as an emotional dare: ShredwithVK 'I bet this transformation will melt your heart' 1.9M at 157x (18 Sep 2026, 28 s).
- A second person on screen: mummy ('Bodybuilder ki mummy' 23.9M, 198x), a stranger to beat, two teenagers vs a powerlifter, a kid.
- Desi price/swap utility: 'Rs 10 weight loss superfood' 1.6M at 41x; 'High protein overnight oats without protein powder' 1.4M at 23x; Rohit Khatri 'Indian budget diet' 13.6x.
- Tiny accounts can and do outlier: channels under 100K subs hit 3x+ on 19.5% of videos vs 12% for 1M+ channels. Rohan Chetri (11.5K), Ani in Gym (13.4K), Ketan Ahuja (4.7K), ITS PUNIT (5.5K), THE VIKFIT (582) all have 5x to 45x videos.
- One-word or emoji titles work when the first frame is the hook ('Close Call 💀' 206x, 'Push up cheat code' 256x, 'One more rep...' 22.6x).

## Doesn't

- 'Secret / truth / reality / exposed' titles under-index (10 top vs 19 bottom): the Indian fitness feed is saturated with them.
- 'I did X for N days' receipt titles under-index as titles (8 vs 15) unless they are a recurring series with a fixed name.
- Generic coach explainers ('How to grow...', 'Best exercise for...') make up most of the bottom third; they work only for channels whose baseline is already built on them.
- Big desi comedy channels barely post gym content now: across Purav Jha, Harsh Beniwal, Round2hell, Jordindian, Raj Grover, Angry Prash and Funcho only 0 to 5% of the last 40 uploads are gym-related. The famous gym sketches (Pankaj Gamer 23.9M, FilterCopy 11.5M, Angry Prash 11.7M) are years old.
- Devanagari-script titles slightly under-index (21 top vs 28 bottom) even on Hindi channels; Roman Hinglish or plain English titles do better.
- Hook length and hookscore.py do not separate winners from losers in this corpus (both 6 words; score 37 vs 39). Do not optimise word count.

## Can work for Srijan (unhinged-funny, 5'5", 70 kg, Pune, Hinglish)

- Viewer-judges with a self-roast payoff: 'Guess my bench' / 'Who is stronger: me or the 60-year-old uncle?' The format is proven (N5) and the loss is the punchline, which suits 'unhinged funny, not expert'. A 5'5", 70 kg guy losing is believable; a jacked coach losing is not.
- That-Short-Height-Guy archetype series: Jay Singh proves the archetype title at 57M; no Indian gym creator owns height jokes. One tripod, one character, many episodes (seat at notch 1, mirror selfie on tiptoe, pull-up bar needs a jump).
- Doubter quotes from his own family and office in Hinglish ('Gym jaake kya ukhad liya?', 'Shaadi ke liye bana raha hai?'): N8 is 15 vs 4 and the family cast (N9) is the biggest desi comedy lever. Keep mummy and papa winning.
- A fixed-name daily series ('Pull-up Arc, Day N' or 'Lean Bulk? Day N'): the smallest channel in the set grows on exactly this, and it gives Srijan a no-think posting habit while his baseline is ~50 views.
- Desi budget maths in his tech voice: price-per-gram-protein tier lists (paneer, soya chunks, eggs, whey) styled like a GPU price/performance chart. Utility gets saves; the tech framing is something only he has.
- Dare-promise reveal flipped into a joke: 'I bet this transformation will make you cry... laughing' with a near-identical before/after.

- Bio line one splits two ways. Funny/relatable creators state the lane as a three-item list ('Funny • Fitness • Lifestyle', 'Entertainment | TRAINER | Bodybuilder', 'Fitness • Acting • Lifestyle'). - Contact: nearly all end with 'DM for collab/promotions' and an email; one prints a personal phone number. At 928 followers Srijan does not need a brand line yet.
