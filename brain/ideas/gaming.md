## Strongest patterns
### Setup, then payoff, in under 15 seconds
Show only the 3–6 s that make the payoff make sense, then the payoff, then 1–2 s of the reaction. No match context, no loading screens. Either start on the setup with a 'watch this' text, or cold-open on the payoff frame and rewind ('wait, how did we get here').

*Why it works:* Completion and rewatches are the top Reels/Shorts signals; a 12 s clip with one clean payoff loops. Viewers only need to understand the stakes, not the game.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=71DYZLHob7I (Protatomonster, 10 s 'right place at the right time'); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ML0rjNI9WyE (Kevvv FPS, 18 s '1 in a MILLION chance!'); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F1udJ_WU03k (FUZE, 13 s 'INSTANT KARMA!!')

### Freeze-frame + meme stinger + 'Bro thought…' caption
Freeze on the frame just before disaster (0.5–1 s, often desaturated or B&W), stamp a caption ('Bro thought he could flash over the wall'), hit one meme sound (record scratch, vine boom), unfreeze into the fail. The 'moments before disaster' grammar.

*Why it works:* The freeze tells the viewer a payoff is coming (open loop) and the caption makes it readable to non-players. One stinger is funny; three are noise.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JexIOA7IJaM (RICHLEV 'Lil Bro Thinks He In a Edit', 1.05M); trend: gaming meme-sound edits (brain/research/trends.json id gaming-meme-sound-edits)

### Zoom/crop to the moment
Punch in 1.3–2x on the crosshair, kill feed, health bar or the friend's character at the payoff; shake 2–4 frames on impact. In vertical, crop to the action rather than letterboxing the whole 16:9 frame.

*Why it works:* Gameplay is tiny on a phone; zoom shows what matters and doubles as emphasis on the beat.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5reb3lTx88 (Hextech Lab Short: tight on the one thing); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4qEdIXLdxMo (Virre CS2)

### Banter is the content: word-timed captions of comms
The friends' voice chat, captioned word by word, carries the joke; the game is the stage. Comms compilations outperform their channels by 10–30x.

*Why it works:* Voices are human and original (nobody else has your friends), and captions make it watchable on mute. Non-players get the joke from the words.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fOvjiAEa3pw (CSGOpoetry comms 1.25M on 41K subs); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KflSJ-2_mpo (StaticMMIV EU comms 938K); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x4d0BYa_4W0 (Salty Boys, Hindi CS2 voice chats)

### Fails beat wins (clutch vs choke)
The choke, the teamflash, the 0.1 s defuse miss and the self-molotov travel further than the ace. Best of all is a clutch that turns into a choke, or the reverse, with the viewer asked to guess.

*Why it works:* Fails are relatable and shareable ('this is you'); wins are only impressive to people who play at that level.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T1_jp1su6bE (Gonzossm 'LEAGUE OF LEGENDS FAIL 2', 11.2M); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j1Hz4T7ct7E (Hitesh KS '0 IQ Players')

### Rage as a physical beat
A facecam rage moment (scream, head in hands, controlled desk tap) timed to the in-game cause, often with a rage meter or phonk drop.

*Why it works:* Physical reaction translates without game knowledge; it is the most clipped streamer moment (CaseOh/Jynxzi).

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hdRC3OnPfD8 (CaseOh desk-slam compilation 3.55M); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0iATr7A5KM (Jynxzi)

### Jump-scare reaction cam
Big facecam (top half or full-bleed with game PiP), 1–3 s of tense silence or breathing, the scare, the scream, then the friend laughing. Often a 2-frame black or white flash on the scare.

*Why it works:* Faces sell fear; the laugh after the scream releases tension and makes it loopable and shareable.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kEJenvt9674 (Live Insaan, 9.4M); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wk6iXvnz2HY (daukey 'try not to giggle in the backrooms', 3.45M)

### Co-op chaos: friends as recurring characters
The same 3–5 friends, each with a role (the betrayer, the coward, the one who reads nothing, the hoarder). Titles frame the group ('4 idiots', 'When Idiots Play', 'Dhokebaaz').

*Why it works:* Series and characters drive follows: viewers come back for the people. Co-op physics games (R.E.P.O.) and proximity chat generate unscripted comedy every session.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=diMdCfiLXCE (shreeman 'Dhokebaaz Ko Mili मौत की सजा', 520K); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rh1n-DqJJDg (GameSprout 'When Idiots Play'); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XzggozInNiw (Terroriser '4 IDIOTS')

### Numbered series and compilations
'FUNNIEST MOMENTS #70', 'CURSED WEAPONS #10', 'TOP 100 #7'. Every part trains the audience to expect the next one.

*Why it works:* Series give the best view-to-follower ratio (Brain atom growth-series-view-to-follower). Small channels get outliers from parts deep in a series (EEZGAME #25 805K on 8K subs).

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nPiNYRXzCeg (LICHTENSTEIN #10 6.67M); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t5aMKYL9SmA (EEZGAME #25)

### Skit inside the game
A stereotype or a character voice acted over gameplay: 'every bronze Yasuo', 'when a girl joins the lobby', a Punjabi uncle character in Valorant, Discord-chat skits.

*Why it works:* Insiders tag friends ('this is literally you'); skits are original by construction.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ld-zKoWUUG4 (TheLoLHounds 'every bronze yasuo'); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2ocKI18AXI (RG's Chillin 'Sukhwinder', 1.4M); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E08lIsyoePc (Beluga, Discord skit about GTA 6)

### Small channels, huge outliers with honest titles
Low-key titles that sound like a friend talking, on clips of their own friend group: 'Backrooms but it's just the moments I found mildly funny'.

*Why it works:* Proof that the algorithm serves good friend-group clips regardless of subscriber count; honesty reads as authentic.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0SPT2lGcE84 (nexus, 2.42M on 4.66K subs); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_Un-XumghSk (Pokeist, 511K on 17K); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZnfXkNmeayM (GutsAndTrenches, 2.42M on 8.6K)

### Vertical layouts for 16:9 gameplay
(A) facecam top third, game bottom two-thirds cropped to the action; (B) full-bleed crop on the action with a small round facecam; (C) blurred-fill background with the full 16:9 frame in the middle and captions above/below (for wide moments like LoL teamfights or the minimap); (D) two POVs stacked (my POV vs friend's POV) for betrayals and screams.

*Why it works:* Each layout keeps the payoff large on a phone. Switching layouts at the beat is itself a pattern interrupt.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IEiaconmVuk (JerryVirus R.E.P.O. Short); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Q_ueARXjas (TheBrownVoid Valorant Short)

### Ride the release calendar with your own footage
Release and event windows (GTA 6 on Nov 19, MW4 launch, LoL Worlds final Nov 14, game updates) get searched and recommended, but the winners use their own angle: a GTA 6 map in another game, trolling the new game's players.

*Why it works:* Topic demand is high, and the viewer still gets something original.

Examples: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lLqJ6oc-kTw (Vanoss 'Gmod Prop Hunt - The GTA6 Map!'); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gM8j5Wyx3Q4 (WILDCAT trolling MW4 players); https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E08lIsyoePc (Beluga 'When GTA 6 Finally Comes Out...')

### Originality rules: reuse is now down-ranked
From Oct 1, 2026 YouTube Shorts recommendations reduce reach for re-uploads of others' content without meaningful additions; YouTube's rep said voice-over, minor edits and template bulk changes don't make a clip original. Instagram already excludes reposts and watermarked clips from recommendations.

*Why it works:* Srijan's advantage is that his clips are his own and his friends'. Compilation aggregators (Red Arcade-style) are exactly what's being squeezed.

Examples: https://9to5google.com/2026/10/02/youtube-wont-recommend-unoriginal-shorts/; https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/; https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/

## What works in India
- Hinglish banter wins: the biggest Indian gaming channels (Techno Gamerz 53.4M, Total Gaming 45.9M, Live Insaan 15.7M) narrate in Hindi/Hinglish. For Instagram, speak Hinglish and caption in Roman Hinglish (or English captions for wider reach) so non-Hindi viewers can follow.
- Indian-server culture is a genre of its own: 'Valorant India is NOT kid friendly', 'Ultimate Mumbai Server Experience', CS2 'Indian lobby' voice chats. Gaali-heavy VC is the joke, but bleep or mute it: reach and brand safety drop with unbleeped abuse.
- Mobile (BGMI, Free Fire) dominates mass Indian gaming; PC games (CS2, Valorant, LoL) are a smaller, older, urban audience, which fits a 26-year-old office worker's account better than kid-skewed Minecraft/Roblox Hindi content (ghost-follower risk, Brain atom growth-follower-first-ghosts).
- Valorant and Fortnite lead Indian social gaming chatter, with BGMI third (Sprinklr data via storyboard18). CS2 has an active Indian community but few good creators (Salty Boys at 8K subs): the lane is open.
- LoL in India is niche (no Indian server; players queue abroad on high ping, our understanding). Make LoL clips universally readable (fails, pings, flame) rather than India-specific, and use English captions for global LoL viewers.
- LAN and gaming-cafe culture: 'gaming cafe' visits, ₹25–₹60/hour PC cafes, LAN events (S8UL/BGMI LANs, STANFEST Bengaluru Oct 31) and the 'friends in one room' energy. A Pune cafe session with friends is a cheap way to film reaction cams with consent.
- Office-gamer identity: '9–6 job, 11 PM ranked', 'standup call after an all-nighter', corporate English vs gaming rage. This ties gaming to Srijan's corporate-guy positioning and to gym/tech pillars.
- Desi reference bank (use as text/voice jokes, not copyrighted clips): Zomato/Swiggy ratings, Pune rent and brokers, Indian dad directions, Maggi at 2 AM, power cuts, chai, 'beta padhai karo', shaadi season, Diwali patakhe (Diwali is Nov 8, 2026). Bollywood dialogue audio is copyrighted: recreate the line in your own voice or use the Instagram library version in-app.
- India release calendar for gaming hooks: GTA 6 preload Nov 12 and launch Nov 19 (console only at launch), LoL Worlds final Nov 14, BGMI Survivor League until Nov 17, MW4 launch window now.
- Post in IST: weekdays 7–9 PM and 10–11:30 PM for gamers (test it), weekends 12–2 PM and 7–10 PM; YouTube long-form Fri/Sat evening.

## Clip-to-reel workflow
**1. Gather and name every clip** (ffprobe, file manager)

Copy all existing clips into one folder tree: Gaming/00_inbox → 01_raw/<game>/ → 02_moments/ → 03_projects/<post-id>/ → 04_exports/ → 99_gold/. Rename raw files as YYYY-MM-DD_game_map-or-mode_people_note.ext, e.g. 2026-10-21_cs2_inferno_srijan-rohit-aman_premier.mkv. Then run a quick audit so you know what each file has (length, resolution, fps, how many audio tracks): for f in 01_raw/*/*; do ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration:stream=index,codec_type,width,height,r_frame_rate -of compact "$f"; done. Files with 2+ audio tracks (game / mic / Discord) are gold: you can caption the banter cleanly.

**2. Find loud moments (screams, laughs, rage) from the audio** (ffmpeg + Python)

Most funny moments are audio spikes on the mic or Discord track. Measure loudness every 0.1 s: ffmpeg -i clip.mkv -map 0:a:0 -af "aresample=48000,asetnsamples=n=4800,astats=metadata=1:reset=1,ametadata=print:key=lavfi.astats.Overall.RMS_level:file=rms.txt" -f null - (use 0:a:1 or 0:a:2 for the mic/Discord track if it exists). Then list every second that is 12 dB louder than the clip's median, merge spikes closer than 3 s, and pad each by 8 s before and 3 s after (the setup comes before the scream). A 10-line Python loop over rms.txt does this; Claude can run it for a whole folder and write moments.csv.

**3. Find laughter and screams specifically** (earshot)

Volume alone also catches gunfire. earshot's listening transcript tags events with AudioSet classes, including Laughter, Giggle, Screaming, Shout and Gunshot: ~/sm-tools/earshot/.venv/bin/python -m earshot timeline clip.mkv --detail high --json --out tl.json, then keep events tagged Laughter/Screaming/Shout. For one long session, ask directly: earshot find mic.wav "a group of friends laughing loudly" --top 10. Laughter after a spike = a confirmed funny moment.

**4. Transcribe the banter (word-timed)** (faster-whisper (tools/transcribe.py))

Extract the voice track (ffmpeg -i clip.mkv -map 0:a:1 -ac 1 -ar 16000 mic.wav) and run faster-whisper with word timestamps: .venv/bin/python tools/transcribe.py mic.wav small (writes mic.wav.words.json). Search the transcript for comedy keywords: 'bhai', 'what', 'no no no', 'kya kar raha', 'bro', 'run', 'bachao', 'sorry', 'gg', laughter. Hinglish caveat: auto-detect often picks Hindi and writes Devanagari; for Roman-script captions rerun with language='en' and hand-fix Hindi words (budget 2–3 min per 30 s clip). The words.json later becomes the captions.

**5. Find kill-feed and visual moments** (ffmpeg scene filter / OpenCV)

For CS2/Valorant/LoL, crop the kill-feed area and detect when it changes: ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf "crop=iw*0.28:ih*0.22:iw*0.72:0,select='gt(scene,0.08)',showinfo" -f null - 2>&1 | grep -o 'pts_time:[0-9.]*' (CS2 kill feed is top-right; LoL's is right-middle; adjust the crop). Three or more kill-feed changes within 6 s = a multi-kill. OpenCV can do the same plus detect the grey 'you died' screen (mean saturation drops) to find your deaths fast.

**6. Shortlist and rate** (spreadsheet + ffmpeg)

Put every candidate in moments.csv: file, start, end, game, people, tag (fail / clutch / choke / betrayal / scream / banter / rage / physics), rating 1–5, used_in. Watch only the shortlisted windows at 1.5x. Keep only 4s and 5s that pass the shareability test: 'who would I send this to, and why?'. Cut them losslessly into 02_moments: ffmpeg -ss 00:12:31 -to 00:12:52 -i clip.mkv -map 0 -c copy 02_moments/2026-10-21_cs2_choke-1v2_r5.mkv (re-encode later in the edit).

**7. Reframe to 9:16** (ffmpeg / CapCut / VN)

Pick a layout per idea. (A) facecam top, game bottom (1080×608 + 1080×1312): ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v]crop=889:1080:515:0,scale=1080:1312[g];[0:v]crop=480:270:0:810,scale=1080:608[f];[f][g]vstack[v]" -map "[v]" -map 0:a -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -r 30 out.mp4 (move the game crop's x to centre the action; the facecam crop assumes a 480×270 cam in the bottom-left). (B) full-bleed action crop: crop=608:1080:X:0,scale=1080:1920 (record at 1440p so the crop stays sharp). (C) blurred fill for wide moments: [0:v]scale=1080:1920:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=1080:1920,boxblur=20[bg];[0:v]scale=1080:-2[fg];[bg][fg]overlay=(W-w)/2:(H-h)/2. (D) two POVs stacked: each crop=1215:1080:X:0,scale=1080:960, then vstack. In CapCut: Canvas 9:16 → duplicate the clip → crop each copy → keyframe Position to follow the action.

**8. Caption the banter** (faster-whisper → CapCut / ffmpeg ass / HyperFrames)

Turn words.json into word-by-word captions (2–4 words on screen, active word highlighted). Fastest on phone: CapCut Auto captions (supports Hindi; check Roman vs Devanagari) and fix words. Local: Claude converts words.json to an .ass subtitle with per-word karaoke highlight and burns it with ffmpeg -vf ass=subs.ass, or HyperFrames renders animated captions. Rules: label who is talking by colour (Srijan white, friend 1 yellow, friend 2 cyan), keep captions in the middle band (above the bottom 350 px and below the top 250 px), bleep and asterisk gaali (b***), and add a translation line in English only when the Hindi joke matters.

**9. Time the meme SFX** (CapCut / Edits / earshot)

One stinger per payoff, never two in a row. Place the stinger on the frame of the payoff (the death, the drop, the scream), duck or cut game audio under it for 0.3–0.6 s, and freeze-frame for 0.5–1 s if the joke needs reading time. Meaning map: record scratch = 'wait, rewind'; vine-boom bass hit = realisation; 'bruh' = disappointment; riser = tension before a scare; cut-to-silence = horror beat. Levels per library/mixing/MIXING.md: meme stings at about voice level, once. Check sync with earshot timeline out.mp4 (offset should be within one frame). Source: earshot's bank for hits/risers/glitches; meme stings must be found (Openverse CC0, or Instagram's in-app sound library).

**10. Music (or no music)** (Edits / CapCut / YouTube Audio Library)

Banter clips: no music, or a bed at −22 to −24 LUFS that drops out on the punchline. Montages: phonk or trending audio cut to the beat. Instagram: add trending audio inside Edits at 5–15% under the original sound, or use a low-use track. YouTube: use YouTube Audio Library or nothing; copyrighted phonk and game OSTs can be Content-ID claimed. Horror: let the game's silence work; add a sub-drop only on the scare.

**11. Mix and loudness** (ffmpeg loudnorm / earshot)

Voice/comms −16 LUFS (anchor), game −20 to −24 LUFS under talking and up to −16 when nobody talks, SFX per MIXING.md, final master −14 LUFS integrated, true peak −1 dBTP. If game and voice are on one track, use CapCut's 'Enhance voice' carefully (it can eat the game). Verify with earshot critique export.mp4 --fast.

**12. Length targets** (—)

Meme payoff: 7–15 s (loops). Story clip with banter: 20–35 s. Mini compilation (5–6 moments, ≤8 s each, numbered): 45–60 s. Explainer-skit (court case, real-estate tour): 35–60 s. YouTube Shorts: same cuts, under 60 s. YouTube long: 8–14 min episodes or monthly compilations with chapters. Cut every dead second; a visual change every 1.2–2.5 s.

**13. Export and post** (Edits / CapCut / YouTube Studio)

1080×1920, 30 fps (60 fps if the game motion matters and the clip is 60 fps), H.264 high bitrate, HDR off. Text inside safe zones. Instagram: post from Edits (no watermark), collab-tag friends who appear, 3–5 hashtags, keyword caption. YouTube Shorts: re-export without Instagram text styling if needed, title = hook line. Cover frame = the freeze-frame with the caption.

**14. Archive what worked** (spreadsheet)

After posting, move the source moment to 99_gold/<game>/ and fill used_in in moments.csv. Every month, unused 4-rated moments become the YouTube compilation, and the top Reel gets a part 2. Log views, sends and saves per post to see which franchise to double down on.

**15. Capture setup going forward (OBS Studio, free)** (OBS Studio + Aitum Vertical + iPhone webcam app)

Settings → Output → Advanced → Recording: format Hybrid MP4 or MKV (remux later), encoder NVENC/AMF (HEVC or H.264), rate control CQP 18–20, keyframe 2 s, preset P5. Video: base = your monitor res, output 1920×1080 (or 2560×1440 for crop headroom), 60 fps. Audio tracks: Track 1 = full mix (for quick posts), Track 2 = game (Application Audio Capture → game .exe, or Desktop Audio), Track 3 = mic, Track 4 = Discord (Application Audio Capture → Discord.exe). Tick tracks 1–4 in Recording, assign in Advanced Audio Properties. Replay buffer: Output → Replay Buffer → enable, 90–120 s, set a 'Save Replay' hotkey (e.g. F9) and start the buffer with every session, so you only press a key after something funny happens. Vertical: the Aitum Vertical plugin (needs OBS 31.1+) adds a 1080×1920 canvas with its own 'backtrack' (replay) hotkey; untick 'Use main OBS settings' and pick tracks or vertical files record silent. Facecam: iPhone 15 on the tripod as a webcam (Camo or Iriun app, free tiers) placed bottom-left at 480×270 in the 16:9 scene so the Step-7 crop recipe works. Fallback: NVIDIA App / AMD Adrenalin instant replay (separate mic track option).

**16. Re-shoot old matches from new angles (game-native replays)** (CS2 demo viewer / LoL replay + OBS)

CS2: download recent matches from Watch → Your Matches, then playdemo <name>, Shift+F2 for the demo UI, demo_timescale 0.25 for slow-mo, cl_draw_only_deathnotices 1 to hide the HUD but keep the kill feed, and spectate the enemy who died to you ('what they saw'). LoL: replays (.rofl) under Documents/League of Legends/Replays play only on the current patch, so re-record any replay you want before the next patch; use the replay camera, hide UI with H (verify hotkeys in your client) and slow time. This turns one old moment into a fresh, original angle.
