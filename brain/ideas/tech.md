## Lane
The funny PC gamer next door from Pune who tests the hype on his own rig. Three formats he can own: (1) 'Ek Setting' one-tip fixes that open on a symptom and prove the result on screen; (2) 'Tech news, translated to FPS and ₹': what a GPU/CPU/Windows change means for an Indian gamer's frames and wallet, rumours labelled; (3) roast tier lists and skits (tips, GPUs, Windows features, shopkeeper POVs). Not a studio PC-build channel like Rahul Hinduja (builds/buying by budget) or a lab like Hardware Unboxed: Srijan's edge is settings + humour + his own rig + Hinglish, and it overlaps with his gaming lane (CS2/LoL clips as proof).

Series used below: **Ek Setting** (one-tip fixes), **Tech News → FPS & ₹**, **Rating the Internet** (tier lists), **Myth ya Fact**, **Pune PC Problems** (skits), **Rig Diaries** (reviews).

## India notes
- Always show ₹ prices with a date stamp and the shop/site (Amazon, Flipkart, mdcomputers, PrimeABGB, Vedant, local shop). GPU prices in India rose ~30% on average Jul→Aug 2026 (RTX 5060 ~₹32.8K → ~₹46K, RTX 5050 ~₹28.8K → ~₹44K per TimesOfGames, 1 Sep 2026, single source). Re-check on shoot day.
- RAM crisis in rupees: 32GB DDR5 kits listed ₹37–44K on Amazon India in Jun–Jul 2026 (Crucial ₹37,475; Patriot Viper ₹43,999). Say 'listing on <date>', not 'price'.
- Sale calendar: Amazon Great Indian Festival from 8 Oct; Flipkart Big Bang Diwali Sale reported from ~28 Oct (dates differ by source, verify in-app); Diwali is 8 Nov 2026. 'Don't buy' and 'is this deal real?' reels peak in the 10 days before Diwali.
- Core budget bands for Indian PC gamers: ₹50–60K entry, ₹75K–1L mainstream, ₹1.5L enthusiast. Laptops outsell desktops; 'gaming laptop' tips have a wider audience than desktop-only tips.
- Hinglish rules: speak Hinglish, keep on-screen text mostly English with Hinglish punchlines in Latin script ('bhai', 'scene', 'paisa vasool'). Commands/paths always in exact English.
- Local context jokes: power cuts and UPS, 38°C summer room temps and dust, '₹500 repair wale bhaiya', office laptop vs home rig, Pune traffic, chai-break analogies. Name Pune shops only if Srijan actually uses them.
- Competitive games with Indian audiences: Valorant, CS2, BGMI (mobile), GTA V RP, LoL smaller. Use CS2/Valorant for latency tips (both support NVIDIA Reflex).
- GST on PC components is 18% and import duties apply, which is why India lands above US MSRP: a fair 'why is it costlier here' explainer line.
- Sponsored or affiliate posts must carry a clear disclosure label (ASCI influencer guidelines: 'Ad'/'Paid partnership' up front). The Brain has no ASCI notes yet (gap in trends.json).
- Indian audiences save and share money-saving and 'don't get scammed' reels most; frame tips as rupees saved or repair visits avoided.

## One-tip bank
Risk: low = settings anyone can undo; medium = BIOS or stability; high = we don't recommend it. Every tip was checked against official docs or reputable testing; benefits are stated without placebo. Nothing here disables Defender, Windows Update, Secure Boot or Memory Integrity.

**10 best for one-tip reels:** `tip-refresh-rate`, `tip-cable-gpu`, `tip-xmp-expo`, `tip-driver-restart`, `tip-laptop-gpu`, `tip-vrr-cap`, `tip-bios-command`, `tip-bottleneck-check`, `tip-sfc-dism`, `tip-battery-report`.

| id | Tip | Command / path | Real benefit | Risk | Myth check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tip-refresh-rate` | Your 144Hz monitor might be running at 60Hz | Settings > System > Display > Advanced display > Choose a refresh rate > pick the highest (e.g. 144/165/240Hz). In-game: set the same refresh and Fullscreen/Borderless. | If it was wrong: motion goes from 60 to 144+ updates per second. Huge smoothness gain and lower display latency, zero cost. If it was already right: nothing changes. | low | Real. Also check the cable: HDMI 1.4 or a cheap cable can cap refresh; use DisplayPort or the cable that came with the monitor. |
| `tip-cable-gpu` | Plug the monitor into the graphics card, not the motherboard | Look at the back of the PC: the display cable must go into the ports on the horizontal GPU bracket (lower), not the vertical motherboard I/O ports (upper). Check: Task Manager > Performance > GPU 0/GPU 1 and see which GPU the game uses. | If it was wrong: often 2–10× more FPS instantly because the game moves from iGPU to the real GPU. | low | Real and very common with first builds. |
| `tip-xmp-expo` | Turn on XMP/EXPO: your RAM is probably running slow | Check: Ctrl+Shift+Esc > Performance > Memory > 'Speed' (e.g. 4800 MT/s vs the 6000 on the box). Fix: reboot into BIOS (shutdown /r /fw /t 0) > enable XMP (Intel) or EXPO (AMD) profile > Save & Exit. | Typically a few % to ~10% in CPU-bound games, better 1% lows; more on Ryzen. Varies by game and CPU. | medium | Real, but update the BIOS first. If the PC fails to boot, it will usually retry with defaults; otherwise clear CMOS (motherboard manual). Not an overclock you need to tune. |
| `tip-game-mode` | Game Mode: what it really does | Settings > Gaming > Game Mode > On (default on). | Small; mainly fewer hitches when background tasks run. Not a magic FPS boost. | low | Old myth said it hurt FPS; that was early Windows 10. On Windows 11 keep it on. |
| `tip-hags` | Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling (HAGS) | Settings > System > Display > Graphics > Advanced/Change default graphics settings > Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling > On > restart (location varies by build). | Usually 0–a few % or none; required for NVIDIA DLSS Frame Generation. Turn on if you use frame gen; otherwise test both. | low | Partly myth as an FPS booster; real as a frame-gen requirement. |
| `tip-windowed-opt` | Optimizations for windowed games | Settings > System > Display > Graphics > Change default graphics settings > Optimizations for windowed games > On. | Lower latency in borderless mode and unlocks Auto HDR/VRR there. Not more FPS. | low | Real (Microsoft support doc). Auto HDR forces it on. |
| `tip-laptop-gpu` | Gaming laptop: make sure the game uses the real GPU | Settings > System > Display > Graphics > select the game (Add desktop app if missing) > GPU preference: High performance. Plus in the vendor app (Armoury Crate/Legion Space/MSI Center/Omen) set GPU mode to Discrete/dGPU (MUX) or Ultimate, and play plugged in. | If the game was on the iGPU: massive gain. MUX/discrete: often ~5–15% more FPS and lower latency (varies by laptop). | low | Real. Switching MUX needs a restart; battery life drops in discrete mode. |
| `tip-driver-restart` | Win+Ctrl+Shift+B restarts your graphics driver | Press Windows + Ctrl + Shift + B together. Screen blinks black and beeps. | Fixes a frozen/black screen or a second monitor that won't wake, in 2 seconds. No FPS change. | low | Real. Doesn't fix a crashed game; it fixes the display path. |
| `tip-rebar` | Check Resizable BAR (Smart Access Memory) | NVIDIA: NVIDIA Control Panel > System Information > Resizable BAR: Yes/No. AMD: Adrenalin > Performance > Tuning > Smart Access Memory. Any GPU: GPU-Z 'Resizable BAR'. To enable: BIOS > Above 4G Decoding On + Re-Size BAR On (CSM off, UEFI boot). | 0–10% depending on the game (NVIDIA whitelists games); essential for Intel Arc cards (big losses without it). | medium | Real but small on most NVIDIA setups. Turning off CSM can break boot on old Legacy/MBR installs: check msinfo32 'BIOS Mode: UEFI' first. |
| `tip-bios-command` | Restart straight into BIOS, no F2 spam | Open Terminal (Admin) > shutdown /r /fw /t 0 | Saves the 'which key, how fast' struggle; useful for XMP/ReBAR/fan curves. | low | Real. Needs UEFI mode (msinfo32 > BIOS Mode). Save your work first: it restarts instantly. |
| `tip-bottleneck-check` | Bottleneck check that actually works | In game: Win+G > Performance widget (or NVIDIA overlay Alt+R / AMD overlay Ctrl+Shift+O). GPU usage ~95–99% = GPU-bound (normal, good). GPU well below ~90% with one CPU thread pegged or low FPS = CPU/engine limit. | Tells you whether lowering graphics settings or a better CPU will help. | low | Myth-bust: online 'bottleneck calculators' guess from part names and ignore the game, resolution and settings. |
| `tip-vrr-cap` | Smoothest combo: VRR on + cap FPS a few below refresh + Reflex | Monitor OSD: FreeSync/Adaptive-Sync on. NVIDIA App > Graphics > Global: G-SYNC on, Max Frame Rate = refresh − 3 (141 on 144Hz); in game turn on NVIDIA Reflex if offered (CS2, Valorant). AMD: FreeSync on in Adrenalin, cap with Radeon Chill (min = max) or the in-game limiter, Anti-Lag on. | Smoother frame pacing and lower input lag than V-Sync alone. Doesn't raise FPS. | low | Real and widely tested (Blur Busters-style guidance). DLSS 4.5 Dynamic MFG currently doesn't work with frame limiters/V-Sync: turn the cap off there. |
| `tip-upscaler-modes` | DLSS/FSR/XeSS modes, decoded | In game graphics settings: Upscaler = DLSS / FSR / XeSS; Mode = Quality / Balanced / Performance / Ultra Performance. | Large FPS gains. At 1440p/4K, Quality/Balanced look close to native; at 1080p, Performance gets soft. | low | 'Upscaling is always blurry' is outdated for DLSS 4.x/FSR 4.x; still true for old FSR 1/2 at 1080p. |
| `tip-frame-gen` | Frame generation: smooth isn't the same as fast | In game: DLSS Frame Generation / Multi Frame Generation (RTX 40/50; 3x–6x on RTX 50), FSR Frame Generation (AMD; ML version on RX 9000), XeSS-FG. | Great for single-player at ≥ ~50–60 base FPS on high-refresh screens. Adds some latency; bad idea in CS2/Valorant. | low | Myth: '240 FPS with frame gen = as responsive as real 240'. It isn't. |
| `tip-dlss-override` | Upgrade old games to the newest DLSS model (NVIDIA App) | NVIDIA App > Graphics > pick the game (or Global) > DLSS Override – Model Presets > Latest/Recommended; then enable DLSS in the game. | Sharper, more stable image in older DLSS titles. Newest presets cost more performance on RTX 20/30. | low | Real, official. Game must be on NVIDIA's override list. |
| `tip-fsr41-old-radeon` | RX 6000/7000 owners: free FSR 4.1 upgrade | Install AMD Software Adrenalin 26.10.1 or newer > open a supported game's profile in Adrenalin > turn on the FSR 4 upgrade/override toggle (name can vary by driver) > choose FSR in game. | Better image quality than FSR 3.1 at the same mode; some performance overhead on older cards (AMD hasn't published RX 6000 numbers). | low | Real (20 Oct 2026). Not on Xbox yet. Frame-gen ML upgrade is still RX 9000 only. |

Small or no FPS change on a strong PC; noticeable on 8GB laptops. | low | Real for boot time; 'more FPS' only if those apps were hogging resources. |
